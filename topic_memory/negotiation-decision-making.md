# Topic Memory: Negotiation & Decision-Making
_Last updated: 2026-11-05_

## Established findings
- Leadership identity shows up in concrete capital-allocation and boundary decisions (make-or-buy, greenfield vs. M&A), not just in governance optics — now supported across two runs and two leadership attributes: board composition (W7203630460, 2026-08-17, Taiwan) and CEO political ideology (W7221038267, 2026-10-07, U.S. panel, firm FE + successions).
- Observers (and researchers) routinely apply linear shortcuts to decision data that the evidence does not support — growth = competence (W7214937827, 2026-09-30); liberal = pro-social (W7224801151, 2026-10-29, U-shaped).

## Emerging threads
- CEO political ideology as a measured strategy input: conservative-leaning CEOs vertically integrate more, moderated by asset specificity and industry concentration (W7221038267). Watch for replication outside U.S. public firms and for direct measurement of ideology rather than donation proxies.
- Moral foundations as the mechanism behind pro-social corporate behavior: both ideological poles act, the middle does least (W7224801151). Watch for experimental tests of "tailor the moral framing to the leader."
- Framing effects on identical economics: revenue gains beat mathematically equivalent cost cuts for competence, rehiring and investor appeal (W7214937827). No follow-up yet.
- Gender as a demand-side variable in face-to-face selling: female sellers drew 41% more customers but also more bargaining and unpaid conversational labor (W7203695857, 2026-08-18). No follow-up yet.
- Emotion as a lever on economic preference: gratitude raised green willingness-to-pay and buffered the money-desire spike caused by climate information (W7213965712, 2026-09-21; intentions only). No follow-up yet.
- Methods caution recurring across runs: psychology is almost always inferred from archival outcomes rather than measured; single-country and single-sector samples are common.

## Feature ideas already pitched
- "The perception tax" (2026-10-06): economically identical choices decided by framing, approachability and room composition (W7214937827 + W7203695857 + W7203630460, with W7213965712). Headlines used: same dollar worth more as growth; cost-cutters save the company and lose the promotion; which side of the ledger you work on.
- "What a CEO's politics really predicts" (2026-11-05): ideology drives make-or-buy strongly but relates to pro-social practice non-linearly, with moderates doing least (W7221038267 + W7224801151). Headlines used: what a CEO's politics really predicts; fence-sitters not partisans; your job description depends on how your CEO votes. Do not re-pitch the generic "politics in the C-suite" frame without new evidence.
