## Emerging threads
- Framing effects on identical economics: revenue gains judged more favorably than mathematically equivalent cost cuts, with spillover to competence ratings, consultant rehiring and investor attraction (W7214937827, 2026-09-30, nine experiments + ~13M-article archival analysis).
- Gender as a demand-side variable in face-to-face selling: matched-stall field experiment in India found female sellers drew 41% more customers but also more bargaining attempts and off-topic conversation (W7203695857, 2026-08-18) — reframes microentrepreneur earnings gaps as structural, not interactional.
- Board composition shows up in concrete capital-allocation choices: new female directors associated with greenfield over M&A foreign entry; effect amplified by a female chair, dampened by internationally experienced incumbents (W7203630460, 2026-08-17; Taiwanese manufacturing, correlational).
- Emotion as a lever on economic preference: gratitude induction raised willingness to pay for eco-labeled goods and buffered the money-desire increase caused by climate information (W7213965712, 2026-09-21; intentions/hypothetical allocations only).
- Cross-cutting: three of four studies locate the decision at the perception layer rather than the economics — worth watching whether later runs produce replication or boundary conditions.

## Feature ideas already pitched
- "The perception tax" (2026-10-06): economically identical choices decided by framing, approachability and room composition (W7214937827 + W7203695857 + W7203630460, with W7213965712 as emotional-framing support); angled at the current efficiency/cost-cutting cycle and at who gets credited for a dollar. Headlines used: same dollar worth more as growth; cost-cutters save the company and lose the promotion; which side of the ledger you work on.
