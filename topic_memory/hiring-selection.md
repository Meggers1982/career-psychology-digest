## Established findings
- Inequality surfaces in design details and daily texture, not stated policy: Brazilian Black workers' lower autonomy/competence/contribution satisfaction (W7224595503); migrant women under scrutiny (W7207613776); disability visibility effects (W7211881958); and now test item wording (W7231573809) and thin applicant files (W7225306551, 2026-11).
- Candidates read employer and process signals as character references: equity (W7204553807), job-ad humor (W7203608858), CSR vs. salary (W7204088664), AI vs. human screening (W7223109819).
- Hiring technology underperforms its sales pitch when tested: filters cut quality applicants (W7203733318); AI rejections redirect blame (W7204209860); AI-interview age-language gaps (W7214200214).
- Validity evidence quality is itself contested: incumbent-based, correction-dependent estimates (W7222969019, 2026-10) vs. meta-analytic defenses showing applicant-sample validity holds or exceeds research samples (W7214648548; W7225104062, 2026-11, job-specific .34 vs. generic .19).

## Emerging threads
- Fairness "fixes" that backfire: criterion weighting toward contextual performance worsens adverse impact (W7225329342); work-contextualized personality items widen autistic/non-autistic score gaps and show measurement bias (W7231573809).
- Explainability as the competitive alternative to black-box AI scoring: transparent word dictionaries with auditable validity and small subgroup gaps (W7225331132, 2026-11).
- Individuating information as a bias-compression mechanism — counterstereotypical warmth/competence detail erased a trans hireability gap (W7225306551).
- Measurement invariance as a precondition for any group-gap claim (W7224595503, W7231573809); most employer data would not pass this bar.
- Non-Western evidence base widening: India, Brazil, Chile, Germany, Pakistan.
