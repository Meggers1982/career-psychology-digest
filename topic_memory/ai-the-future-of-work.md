# Topic Memory: AI & the Future of Work
_Last updated: 2026-11-08_

## Established findings
- Algorithmic management functions as identity and normative control, not just scheduling: reputation scoring makes worker self-worth and earning capacity contingent on an unappealable system (W7213334889 2026-09-15; W7218741843 2026-10-03; W7224565098 2026-10-29).
- Resistance to AI at work is about authority and moral standing, not competence: AI accepted as subordinate, rejected as leader (W7168435596 2026-09-21); outsiders penalize firms that give AI the final say in HR (W7204855159 2026-08-31).
- Social support is not a structural fix: peer support dampened the motivational benefit of fair-seeming algorithms (W7204647820 2026-08-29) and helped freelancers cope without changing platform pay, ranking or voice (W7224565098 2026-10-29).
- Whether AI exposure mobilizes or paralyzes careers depends on individual disposition/resources — vivid future work self (W7223279808 2026-10-23), proactive personality (W7225175362 2026-11-03) — while the paralyzing case is also documented (W7213541995 2026-09-18).
- Methodological caution: field dominated by three-wave self-report surveys and single-country qualitative studies (UK, China, India, Italy, Sri Lanka, Canada); causal and cross-national evidence remains thin.

## Emerging threads
- Technostress as a universal cost that cuts across personality and predicts withdrawal/quiet quitting regardless of who benefits from AI (W7225175362).
- Quiet quitting reframed as a resource-protection response to technology rollouts rather than a generational attitude (W7225175362).
- Generative AI skill devaluation as a present-tense income event in freelance knowledge markets, not an anticipated threat (W7224565098) — extends algorithmic-management research beyond delivery/ride-hailing.
- Identity work, not tool training, as the hard part of AI adoption in creative/knowledge occupations: resisting, extending, crafting (W7220851400 2026-10-23).
- Remote-capable, digitally intensive work as a new inequality axis; immigrants concentrated in digital but low-telework-feasibility industries (W7222280836 2026-10-15).

## Feature ideas already pitched
- 2026-10-24: "Authority, not accuracy" — resistance and reputational/service damage stem from giving machines the final word; human-in-the-loop as the cheap design fix (W7168435596, W7204855159, W7213334889, W7218741843, W7204647820). Pitched to MIT SMR, HBR, Wired, The Atlantic.
- 2026-11-08: "The privatization of AI adaptation" — cost of adjusting to AI shifted onto individuals; works only for the already-proactive or well-networked, while technostress and skill devaluation hit everyone (W7225175362, W7224565098). Pitched to Fast Company, Rest of World, Business Insider, The Guardian.
