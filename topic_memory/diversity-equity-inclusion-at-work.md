# Topic Memory: Diversity, Equity & Inclusion at Work
_Last updated: 2026-11-04_

## Established findings
- Symbolic DEI levers underperform their reputation: gender-fair pronouns don't shift leader stereotypes (W7171908932, 2026-09); "Disability Confident" accreditation rarely appears as concrete commitments in job ads (W7212427136, 2026-09); firms reconfigure HRM plumbing while changing public language (W7212220471, 2026-09). Watch practices, not labels.
- Business-case framing of diversity is both empirically weak and socially counterproductive: little organization-level evidence for neurodiversity benefits (W7225083047, 2026-11); employees read business-case justifications as marketing and respond with cynicism or defensiveness (W7214634718, 2026-09).
- Resistance from majority-group gatekeepers is the recurring obstacle, and it is patterned: "fit" critiques, gatekeeping, non-compliance (W7222236166, 2026-10); national-history-shaped objections among white male managers (W7197013324, 2026-08).
- Resources and structure, not titles or messaging, carry the equity effect: men's authority comes with budget/staff, driving meritocracy beliefs (W7203448124, 2026-08); networks only bite with policy access (W7203758314, 2026-08); infrastructure beats exhortation in behavior-change meta-analysis (W7213915259, 2026-09).

## Emerging threads
- Disability/neurodiversity research moving upstream from post-hire accommodation (W7214278713) and advert signaling (W7212427136) to hiring-process design itself (W7225167352, 2026-11) and to managerial processes that convert individual capacity into firm value (W7225083047).
- Work–family policy built on a two-shift model under-serves extended-family and community care ("third shift," W7213356687, 2026-09) — intersectional caregiving as a distinct policy gap.
- Outside-work ties cut both ways: off-the-job embeddedness accelerates exit under motherhood discrimination in Japan (W7212085651) vs. family support tracking with sustained STEM interest in Latin America (W7225089427, 2026-11); mentorship showed no clear association in the latter.
- Perceived *visibility* of supportive policy may matter even where symbolic signals fail (W7225089427) — tension with the badge/language findings; needs causal testing.
- AI in selection: recruiters report distrusting algorithms but are moved more by them than by human experts (W7218392398, 2026-10). No new evidence this run.
- Coping-focused interventions for discriminated-against employees (W7196926461, W7211977299) — ongoing burden-shifting critique.
- Methodological note: Nov 2026 batch dominated by reviews and conceptual frameworks rather than experiments; field consolidating faster than it is testing.

## Feature ideas already pitched
- 2026-10-05: "Inclusion theater vs. structural levers" — symbolic DEI (language, badges, workshops) failing while resource allocation, portable accommodations and policy access do the work; pitched to MIT Sloan Management Review, Fast Company, The Atlantic, Fortune.
- 2026-11-04: "The unlabeled era of inclusion" — DEI dissolving into hiring-process design, job design and leave definitions as the business case collapses, with nobody measuring outcomes; pitched to Harvard Business Review, Business Insider, WSJ Work & Careers, The Guardian.
