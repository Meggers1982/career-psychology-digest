# Topic Memory: Career Development & Mobility
_Last updated: 2026-10-30_

## Established findings
- Job crafting is not a neutral well-being lever: in unstable or ambiguous roles it becomes draining identity work (W7221798889, 2026-10), its payoff depends on employee resources like mindfulness and self-efficacy (W7223306094, 2026-10), and only concrete task-level crafting is recognized by supervisors (W7221245981, 2026-10).
- Career competencies and self-knowledge translate into subjective career success only through enacted career crafting (W7222470451, 2026-10).
- Curvilinear patterns recur in career decisions: U-shaped firm performance and CEO retirement (W7223031859, 2026-10); inverted-U job complexity and occupational turnover intent via identity threat (W7222847257, 2026-10). Mid-range conditions are the "sticky"/risky middle.

## Emerging threads
- Occupational/professional identity as the mediating mechanism in mobility decisions (W7222847257, W7221798889) — worth watching whether it generalizes beyond self-report designs.
- "Career inaction" as a distinct research object: wanting an internal move and never making it, via configurational pathways (W7223284391, 2026-10). Small-N qualitative so far.
- Belief systems about how advancement works (meritocratic vs. institutional vs. relational-structural) as an upstream driver of engagement and trust, segmented by gender and age (W7221331977, 2026-10).
- Non-work spillover into workplace risk-taking and voice, e.g., daily investment gains/losses (W7214167465, 2026-09).
- Community-based organizations as informal labor-market intermediaries for immigrants, with social activity engagement outperforming formal career programming (W7223147797, 2026-10).
- Most of this batch is single-country, self-report, cross-sectional; flag replication caution in future runs.
