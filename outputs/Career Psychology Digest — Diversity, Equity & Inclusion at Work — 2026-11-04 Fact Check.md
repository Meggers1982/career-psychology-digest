### Study 1: Inclusive leaders meet quiet resistance — and the pushback has a predictable shape
**Work ID:** W7222236166 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Who nominated the leaders** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The counter-strategies here come from leaders peers nominated as genuinely inclusive, not from a training vendor"
- **Abstract says:** "Drawing on an inductive study of 47 historically marginalized STEM employees and 14 leaders they identified as inclusive"
- **Problem:** The leaders were identified as inclusive by the historically marginalized employees in the study, not by the leaders' peers; 'peers nominated' misstates the source of the nomination.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'leaders whom historically marginalized employees themselves identified as inclusive'."

**Issue B. Strategy label strengthened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "interrogating fit critiques"
- **Abstract says:** "evaluating fit critiques"
- **Problem:** 'Interrogating' implies a more adversarial strategy than the abstract's 'evaluating'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's term, 'evaluating fit critiques'."

### Study 3: In Latin America, family backing and visible policy track with women's STEM ambition despite discrimination
**Work ID:** W7225089427 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample described as women** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "In Latin America, family backing and visible policy track with women's STEM ambition despite discrimination"
- **Abstract says:** "examines science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) career interest among 9,732 STEM and non-STEM students and professionals in Bolivia, Brazil, and Peru. Among 1,139 respondents whose policy-related adversity and gender-discrimination scores both exceeded the response midpoint"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the sample and the high-barrier subset as 'respondents' and 'students and professionals'; it does not say the analysis was restricted to women. The headline presents the result as a finding about women specifically.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'respondents reporting high gender discrimination' or verify the gender composition of the subset before attributing the finding to women."

**Issue B. Causal/comparative wording in story angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "at least in these three countries, who is in your corner at home mattered as much as who is in your corner at work"
- **Abstract says:** "Adjusted analyses showed positive conditional associations for both supports; evidence for their interaction and an association with mentor presence remained inconclusive."
- **Problem:** 'Mattered' implies an effect from a cross-sectional, self-report, nonprobability dataset, and the abstract reports no magnitude comparison between family support and mentor presence (the mentor result was inconclusive, not null).
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'family support was associated with interest, while the evidence on mentor presence was inconclusive — not the same as showing mentors do not help'."

### Study 4: Everyone cites the "business case" for neurodiversity. The evidence for it is thin.
**Work ID:** W7225083047 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Characterization of the literature not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They found a growing body of claims about advantages such as distinctive skills and innovation, but little organization-level empirical evidence, with research unevenly spread and dominated by small qualitative and individual-level work."
- **Abstract says:** "existing research provides a growing body of insights into potential organizational benefits. However, empirical evidence at the organizational level remains limited and uneven... highlighting the need for more quantitative studies assessing organizational-level outcomes and qualitative research unpacking emergence processes"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not name 'distinctive skills and innovation' as the claimed advantages, nor does it say the literature is dominated by 'small' qualitative or individual-level studies — that is an inference from the call for more quantitative organizational-level work. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Trim to 'a growing body of insights into potential organizational benefits, but limited and uneven evidence at the organizational level', and drop the 'small qualitative' characterization unless sourced from the full text."

### Study 5: Beyond "keep it" or "kill it": three ways companies are actually handling the DEI backlash
**Work ID:** W7212220471 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsourced specifics of the three approaches** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "organizations are quietly reconfiguring HRM systems — renaming functions, folding diversity goals into talent or performance processes, or shifting what is said publicly versus what is practiced internally."
- **Abstract says:** "we offer three approaches—all of which are substantiated with evidence from contemporary organizations—that range in the extent to which organizations resist or embrace anti-DEI pressures... contingencies (i.e., external alignment) and configurations (i.e., internal alignment)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe renaming functions, folding diversity goals into talent processes, or public-versus-internal divergence; these specific tactics cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe the three approaches at the level the abstract supports (ranging from resisting to embracing anti-DEI pressure, with attention to external and internal alignment), or attribute the specifics to the full text."

**Issue B. US context claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The analysis is centered on the US political context"
- **Abstract says:** "(no country specified)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that the analysis is US-centered. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'the anti-DEI pressures described are largely those of the current US debate' or confirm from the full text."

### Study 6: The "third shift": why Latina workers carry a load most workplace policies don't see
**Work ID:** W7213356687 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Ranking of missing resources** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The review points to supervisor support as the resource most often missing, which is a management behavior rather than a benefits budget line."
- **Abstract says:** "These challenges are often compounded by limited access to key job resources, including flexibility, supervisor support, and culturally responsive workplace relationships."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists the three resources without ranking them; singling out supervisor support as 'most often missing' adds a priority the review does not state.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the review names three commonly missing resources — flexibility, supervisor support and culturally responsive relationships — one of which is a management behavior rather than a budget line."
