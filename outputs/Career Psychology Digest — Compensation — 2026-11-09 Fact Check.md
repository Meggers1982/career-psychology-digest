### Study 1: How "the minimum wage becomes the maximum wage": inside the audit games of global fashion supply chains
**Work ID:** W7223655616 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Third-party organizations characterized more specifically than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "75 interviews with people at 68 organizations — apparel brands, their suppliers, and third-party auditors and NGOs — all nominally working on worker well-being in global fashion supply chains"
- **Abstract says:** "Our data came from 75 interviews with 68 brands, suppliers, and third-party organizations pursuing worker well-being in global fashion supply chains."
- **Problem:** The abstract says only "third-party organizations"; it does not specify that these were auditors and NGOs. The composition may be broader (e.g., certifiers, industry bodies, consultants). Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "third-party organizations" or add "such as" hedging if the full text supports it."

**Issue B. Unstated detail about how shortfalls are instrumentalized** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "shortfalls against the bigger goal are quietly left unspoken or repackaged as evidence that more monitoring is needed, closing a self-reinforcing loop"
- **Abstract says:** "Self-silencing about this substitution and the instrumentalizing of shortfalls close the configuration into a self-reinforcing loop."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers only to "instrumentalizing of shortfalls" without specifying that shortfalls are recast as justification for more monitoring. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say shortfalls are "put to instrumental use" rather than naming a specific use, unless the full text confirms the monitoring framing."
