### Study 3: When men's scores vary more, the selection test picks a workforce that's both mostly male and weaker
**Work ID:** W7207756322 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Selection-design specifics not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "examine how your cut scores, top-down ranking and banding rules interact with the distribution — not just the average — of candidate scores"
- **Abstract says:** "The supplied abstract text only says measures "are used as selection tools" and that "selection systems with particular (and not uncommon) attributes result in a workforce that is not only mostly male but also inferior with regard to expected job performance.""
- **Problem:** The named design features (cut scores, top-down ranking, banding) and the phrase "when such measures are used to select the top scorers" are specific mechanisms the truncated abstract does not state. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the attributes of your selection system" or note explicitly that the specific design features are inferred from the paper's framing rather than stated in the available abstract."

**Issue B. Comparative claim added** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "yield a workforce that is not only predominantly male but also lower in expected job performance than alternatives"
- **Abstract says:** ""result in a workforce that is not only mostly male but also inferior with regard to expected job performance""
- **Problem:** "than alternatives" adds an explicit comparison benchmark the abstract does not specify.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "than alternatives" or write "lower in expected job performance than the system's designers assume.""

### Study 7: The IQ test's grip on hiring just got weaker: new meta-analysis puts the validity at .20, not .50
**Work ID:** W7203815879 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped moderator nuance ('alone' and the Data×Things combination)** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "contradicting the long-standing assumption that intelligence tests matter most in cognitively demanding jobs"
- **Abstract says:** ""indicated little relationship between Data (cognitive complexity) or People (interpersonal) and validity, a positive relationship for Things (manual), and higher validity in jobs combining greater Data and Things, challenging the traditional view that GMA is most predictive in jobs that are high on cognitive complexity alone""
- **Problem:** The abstract challenges the view that GMA predicts best in jobs high on cognitive complexity *alone*, and reports higher validity where Data and Things combine. The digest omits both the combination finding and the word 'alone', turning a qualified challenge into a flat contradiction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that validity was higher in jobs combining cognitive and manual complexity, and rephrase as "challenging the view that GMA matters most in jobs high on cognitive complexity alone.""

**Issue B. 'People' dimension omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Moderator analyses using O*NET job complexity dimensions found little link between cognitive complexity ("Data") and validity, but a positive relationship for manual complexity ("Things")"
- **Abstract says:** ""little relationship between Data (cognitive complexity) or People (interpersonal) and validity""
- **Problem:** The third O*NET dimension (People/interpersonal), which also showed little relationship, is left out of the summary.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mention that neither Data nor People related to validity."

### Study 13: Being bossed by an algorithm may actually help gig workers switch off at home
**Work ID:** W7204867954 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator described as harm rather than attenuation** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The benefit faded for workers high in "depletion sensitivity," who tire quickly and found the same system draining."
- **Abstract says:** "As gig workers' depletion sensitivity increases, the positive indirect effect of algorithmic management on positive work-family spillover and its negative indirect effect on negative work-family spillover, are both attenuated."
- **Problem:** Attenuation of an indirect benefit is not the same as the system becoming draining or harmful for that subgroup; the abstract reports no reversal of sign. The same overstatement appears in 'while hurting the subset of people who exhaust easily.'
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the benefit was weaker (attenuated) for workers high in depletion sensitivity, rather than that they found the system draining or were hurt by it."

**Issue B. Sample nationality not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Chinese researchers surveyed 658 gig workers across three time points"
- **Abstract says:** "By collecting three-wave time-lagged survey data from 658 gig workers, this study tested the hypotheses."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state the country of the researchers or the gig workers. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the nationality claim or attribute it to the full text rather than the abstract."

### Study 14: When the economy breaks, employees start caring a lot more about whether pay is fair
**Work ID:** W7201985959 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Other commitment drivers overstated as losing power** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests that during downturns, the usual levers of loyalty lose power and distributive fairness becomes the one that matters most"
- **Abstract says:** "only reward fairness increased in predictive importance after the crisis, whereas other determinants remained stable or weakened"
- **Problem:** Other determinants 'remained stable or weakened'; saying they lose power and that fairness becomes the single most important lever goes beyond a relative increase in one predictor within one manufacturing subsidiary.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: reward fairness was the only driver that gained predictive importance, while others stayed flat or weakened."

### Study 21: Cadets who were happy at work two years earlier were likelier to win scarce leadership posts
**Work ID:** W7212286790 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incomplete reporting of non-significant subcomponents** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Affective organizational commitment was the component that most clearly predicted selection; job satisfaction did not."
- **Abstract says:** "Among the subcomponents, affective organizational commitment significantly predicted leadership selection (OR = 1.77, 95% CI [1.07, 2.94]), whereas engagement (OR = 1.63, 95% CI [1.00, 2.66]) and job satisfaction (OR = 1.34, 95% CI [0.85, 2.14]) did not reach the conventional 0.05 significance threshold."
- **Problem:** Naming only job satisfaction as a non-predictor implies engagement did predict selection, when it also failed to reach the significance threshold — and the digest's manager angle specifically points readers to engagement surveys.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write: 'Affective organizational commitment was the only subcomponent to reach significance; engagement and job satisfaction did not.'"

### Study 22: Organizational support only pays off in performance when the manager converts it into motivation
**Work ID:** W7202072432 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated description of low transformational leadership condition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "when it was low, the support-to-empowerment link essentially disappeared"
- **Abstract says:** "transformational leadership strengthens the relationship between POS and psychological empowerment, such that the indirect effects of POS on in-role performance through meaning, competence, and self-determination are significant when transformational leadership is high, but not when it is low"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that the indirect effects on performance were non-significant at low transformational leadership; it does not say the POS–empowerment link itself vanished.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the indirect effects of support on performance were not statistically significant when transformational leadership was low."

**Issue B. Performance rating source not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Support predicted supervisor-rated performance through employees' sense of competence and self-determination."
- **Abstract says:** "We test a multilevel moderated mediation model using multisource and multi-time data from 217 employees and 41 supervisors. Results indicate that POS is positively related to in-role performance through the psychological empowerment dimensions of competence and self-determination."
- **Problem:** The abstract says the data were multisource and included supervisors but does not explicitly state that in-role performance was supervisor-rated. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'in-role performance (multisource data including 41 supervisors)' unless the full text confirms supervisor ratings."

**Issue C. Single-setting claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "context of a single organizational setting limits generalization"
- **Abstract says:** "We test a multilevel moderated mediation model using multisource and multi-time data from 217 employees and 41 supervisors."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no information about whether the sample came from one organization, one industry or one country. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with a caveat that the abstract does not report the sample's organizational or national context."

### Study 23: Employees sent abroad come home to a company that has moved on — and many start looking elsewhere
**Work ID:** W7215123713 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Quantifier in headline not supported by qualitative design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Employees sent abroad come home to a company that has moved on — and many start looking elsewhere"
- **Abstract says:** "Based on semi-structured interviews with 35 repatriates across industries and focus groups with HR professionals... These discrepancies affected well-being, organizational commitment, and retention intentions."
- **Problem:** 'Many start looking elsewhere' implies a prevalence estimate; a 35-person interview study reports themes about retention intentions, not proportions or actual turnover.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to something like '— and some start rethinking whether to stay' or 'and retention intentions suffer'."
