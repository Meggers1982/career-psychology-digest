### 1. Pay gaps inside the executive suite boost digital transformation — until they don't

**Journal:** *Personnel Review* | **Published:** 2026-10-19
**Work ID:** W7222968425 | **DOI:** 10.1108/pr-03-2026-0450

**The study:** Researchers analyzed panel data on 3,583 Chinese A-share listed firms from 2011 to 2023 (37,274 firm-year observations), measuring each company's digital transformation with a text-based index built from annual reports and estimating fixed-effects models with robustness and endogeneity checks. Equity-based incentive exposure for executives and internal pay dispersion were both positively associated with digital transformation, while cash compensation showed a weaker, specification-sensitive link to broad transformation but a clearer link to immediate technology spending. Crucially, pay dispersion showed an inverted-U pattern: moderate gaps between top and lower executive pay tracked with more transformation, very large gaps with less.

**Why it matters:** Boards routinely debate cash versus equity and how wide internal pay gaps should be; this suggests the answer depends on the time horizon of the strategy being funded, and that tournament-style pay has a ceiling beyond which coordination suffers.

**Story angles:**
- **For working professionals and managers:** For executives and compensation committees: audit whether your incentive mix matches the payoff horizon of your strategic bets — cash for near-term resource mobilization, equity for multi-year transformations — and treat internal pay spread as a dial with an optimum rather than a lever to maximize. Note this is correlational panel evidence from one market, so treat it as a hypothesis to test against your own firm's history.
- **For general readers:** The finding offers a window into why the gap between a CEO's pay and that of the executives below them is not just a fairness question: past a point, the spread appears to correspond with worse coordination on big company-wide projects, not better effort.

**Caveats:** Observational panel data — associations, not established causation, despite endogeneity checks; single country (China) and listed firms only; digital transformation measured indirectly through text analysis of annual reports, which may capture corporate rhetoric as much as real change; cash compensation result was sensitive to model specification.

## Research Trends & Continuity

**Convergent findings**
- Both digests converge on the limits of executive incentive pay as a lever for long-horizon corporate goals. "Tying CEO pay to climate goals was linked to more emissions, not fewer" (W7215124307) found that bonusing executives on a strategic non-financial objective did not track with real progress; "Pay gaps inside the executive suite boost digital transformation — until they don't" (W7222968425) similarly finds that cash compensation has only a weak, specification-sensitive link to broad transformation, with a clearer link only to immediate technology spending. The shared implication: short-horizon pay instruments buy short-horizon activity.
- Both digests treat pay level and pay structure — not job content — as the compensation variable that does the real work. "Poverty-level pay pushes workers toward the exit" (W7211910578) isolated pay-related underemployment, rather than field or hours mismatch, as what corrodes satisfaction and retention; the new study locates the action in equity exposure and internal pay dispersion rather than in total compensation.

**Advances**
- The new study moves the executive-pay question from "does incentive pay work?" to "which instrument, on what time horizon?" Where W7215124307 effectively delivered a null-to-negative verdict on incentive-linked pay, W7222968425 (Work ID W7222968425) disaggregates the package — equity versus cash versus internal spread — and finds equity associated with multi-year transformation. That is a genuine refinement of the previous digest's skepticism rather than a contradiction of it.

**Counterpoints**
- The two digests pull in opposite directions on internal pay inequality. The previous digest's framing treated pay shortfalls and pay-related grievance as straightforwardly corrosive (W7211910578); the new study finds that moderate pay dispersion inside the executive suite tracks with *more* digital transformation, with harm appearing only at the extremes (W7222968425). The reconciliation — dispersion as a dial with an optimum, not a monotonic good or evil — is itself the story.

**New themes**
- Tournament theory and internal pay spread enter this topic for the first time (W7222968425); the previous digest contained nothing on vertical pay gaps within management ranks.
- Compensation as a funder of technology strategy, rather than as a motivation or fairness question, is new to the topic.
