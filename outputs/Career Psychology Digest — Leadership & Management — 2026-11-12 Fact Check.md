### Study 1: The business case for psychological safety rests on shakier evidence than you think
**Work ID:** W7224176243 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Added detail about who rated what** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "82% of coded entries measured the leader's behavior through follower ratings — the same people who also rated their own psychological safety — making the evidence causally indeterminate at the point of measurement"
- **Abstract says:** "82% of coded entries operationalize the leader antecedent through follower ratings, rendering the evidence causally indeterminate at the point of measurement"
- **Problem:** The abstract says the leader antecedent was rated by followers and that this makes the evidence causally indeterminate, but it does not state that the same respondents also supplied the psychological safety ratings. That is a plausible inference (and the likely source of the indeterminacy) but it is not in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "measured the leader's behaviour through follower ratings, which the authors say leaves the evidence causally indeterminate at the point of measurement" — or mark the common-rater point as the reviewers' likely concern rather than a stated finding."

**Issue B. Scale hyperbole in reader angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "how a whole research field can accumulate thousands of correlations without ever testing cause and effect"
- **Abstract says:** "conducting the first systematic causal audit of 47 articles published in highly ranked peer-reviewed journals between 1990 and January 2026"
- **Problem:** The audit covers 47 articles in highly ranked journals; "thousands of correlations" is an unverifiable embellishment from the abstract's standpoint.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "how a fast-growing literature can pile up associations without ever testing cause and effect.""

**Issue C. Recommendation attributed to the review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and suggests building in pre/post measurement or staggered rollout so your own program generates better evidence than the published literature"
- **Abstract says:** "We offer theoretical and methodological recommendations for future research, provide a prescriptive roadmap with five pathways toward a more causally rigorous research program, and introduce a diagnostic flowchart"
- **Problem:** The abstract mentions a five-pathway roadmap and a diagnostic flowchart but does not specify pre/post measurement or staggered rollout, and the recommendations are aimed at researchers rather than programme buyers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the specific tactics to the digest ("one practical implication would be…") or note that the roadmap's five pathways are aimed at researchers; the contents are unverifiable from the abstract alone."

### Study 2: Women brace for more disappointment after criticism — unless the feedback is warmly framed
**Work ID:** W7225268086 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies the wrong mechanism** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Women brace for more disappointment after criticism — unless the feedback is warmly framed"
- **Abstract says:** "This gender disparity disappears when feedback is communicated in a communal tone as men increase in their self-disappointment."
- **Problem:** The headline reads as though communal framing spares women the extra disappointment, whereas the gap closes because men's disappointment rises. The body text corrects this, but the headline on its own points the reader in the wrong direction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Try "Women expect more disappointment after criticism — warm framing closes the gap by making men feel worse.""
