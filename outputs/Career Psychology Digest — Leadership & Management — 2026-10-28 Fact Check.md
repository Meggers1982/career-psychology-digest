### Study 2: Negative feedback sparks ideas but kills initiative — on the same day
**Work ID:** W7223046493 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal/absolute headline for a diary study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Negative feedback sparks ideas but kills initiative — on the same day"
- **Abstract says:** "negative feedback can positively influence idea generation through reflection on negative feedback and inspiration. Conversely, it can also negatively impact taking charge through reflection on negative feedback and negative affect"
- **Problem:** "Kills initiative" overstates what is an indirect, within-person association from a 15-day diary study at one firm, and the abstract's hedged "can" is dropped. The body text and caveats are appropriately hedged, so the headline is out of step with them.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like "Negative feedback sparks ideas but can dampen initiative — on the same day"."

### Study 3: When bosses ask for honest criticism, politics turns it into flattery
**Work ID:** W7223396033 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When bosses ask for honest criticism, politics turns it into flattery"
- **Abstract says:** "when perceived organizational politics is high, subordinates are more likely to attribute leaders' feedback-seeking to image enhancement motives, which in turn are associated with greater upward ingratiation"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports associations from three-wave survey data; "turns it into" asserts causation. The caveat flags correlational design, but the headline still reads as causal.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "...politics is linked to more flattery" or "...flattery often follows"."

**Issue B. Outcome not measured** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "producing flattery that leaves the leader more poorly informed than before"
- **Abstract says:** "attempts to solicit honest input may instead be met with ingratiatory responses"
- **Problem:** The study measured upward ingratiation, not the quality or quantity of information leaders received; the claim that leaders end up worse informed is an inference not tested in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the request can yield ingratiation instead of candid input, without asserting a net information loss."

**Issue C. Untested comparative claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The study suggests explicitly signaling developmental intent — and working on the political climate — matters more than the frequency of asking."
- **Abstract says:** "merely encouraging leaders to seek negative feedback is insufficient. Leaders should explicitly signal developmental intent to minimize image enhancement attributions, and organizations should reduce perceived organizational politics"
- **Problem:** The abstract says encouragement alone is insufficient and recommends signalling intent; it does not compare the effect of signalling against frequency of feedback-seeking.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the authors argue that encouraging leaders to ask is not enough on its own — developmental intent needs to be signalled explicitly"."

### Study 4: Impulsive bosses aren't automatically bad bosses — if followers see them as ethical
**Work ID:** W7223936690 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested selection/development comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "screening out impulsivity may matter less than building the ethical context in which it is interpreted"
- **Abstract says:** "the findings indicate that the impact of impulsivity can be conditional and context-dependent... its effects are contingent on ethical leader behavior"
- **Problem:** The study shows moderation by ethical leader behaviour but makes no comparison between selecting on impulsivity and cultivating ethical context, so the relative-importance advice is the digest's extrapolation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as a question for practice ("raises the question of whether ethical context deserves as much attention as trait screening") rather than a conclusion."
