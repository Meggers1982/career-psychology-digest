### Study 1: Should employers screen job applicants for well-being? A warning about where that leads
**Work ID:** W7212036280 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attribution of the four alternatives** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They sketch four safer routes instead: structured assessment of person–environment fit, assessing an applicant's likely effect on colleagues' well-being, measuring how candidates construe ambiguous work situations, and selecting on health behaviors rather than health status."
- **Abstract says:** "we elaborate on two indirect pathways already noted in the provocation article: (a) the structured assessment of person–environment fit and (b) applicants' potential impact on others' well‐being and mental health in the workplace. In addition, we introduce two complementary approaches: (c) assessing how applicants interpret work situations (situation construal)... and (d) selecting for health behaviors"
- **Problem:** The digest presents all four routes as the commentary authors' own proposals, whereas the abstract says the first two were already noted by König et al. and only the latter two are newly introduced.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that two of the four routes elaborate on pathways König et al. already raised, while situation construal and health behaviors are the commentary's own additions."

**Issue B. Characterization of the target article's claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "who argued that employee well-being should be treated as an explicit outcome that personnel selection is designed to maximize"
- **Abstract says:** "advocating for the explicit inclusion of employee well‐being as an outcome in personnel selection"
- **Problem:** 'Designed to maximize' is a stronger formulation than the abstract's 'explicit inclusion… as an outcome'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to 'who argued that employee well-being should be explicitly included as an outcome in personnel selection'."

**Issue C. 'Situation construal' described as ambiguous situations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "measuring how candidates construe ambiguous work situations"
- **Abstract says:** "assessing how applicants interpret work situations (situation construal)"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to work situations generally; 'ambiguous' is an added qualifier not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'ambiguous' and write 'how candidates interpret work situations (situation construal)'."

### Study 2: What actually gets people from a salaried job to full-time freelancing — and what keeps them afloat
**Work ID:** W7204183347 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Financial' support not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "close personal ties — family and friends rather than professional contacts — absorbed the financial and emotional shocks that made the career sustainable"
- **Abstract says:** "bonding social capital buffers transition-related risks through emotional and practical support, thereby promoting career sustainability"
- **Problem:** The abstract says 'emotional and practical support'; the digest converts 'practical' into specifically 'financial' shocks, and specifies the bonding ties as family and friends, neither of which the abstract states.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's wording — 'absorbed transition risks through emotional and practical support' — and describe bonding social capital as close personal ties without asserting the specific composition."

**Issue B. Why it matters leans on an unstated inference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests the practical precondition for going independent is not just skill or a client list but a tight private support network that can absorb the bad months — a resource distributed very unequally."
- **Abstract says:** "bonding social capital buffers transition-related risks through emotional and practical support, thereby promoting career sustainability"
- **Problem:** 'Practical precondition' and the inequality-of-access claim go beyond a nine-person interpretive study; the abstract makes no distributional claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'these nine accounts suggest that close personal support, not just skill or clients, helped sustain the transition' and drop or explicitly flag the inequality point as commentary."
