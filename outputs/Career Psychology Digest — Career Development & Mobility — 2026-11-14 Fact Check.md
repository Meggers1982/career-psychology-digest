### Study 1: A sense of calling isn't found once — it's rebuilt every few months
**Work ID:** W7221320159 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderation interval dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Employees with high core self-evaluation got more calling out of strengths-based job crafting, but less additional benefit from an inclusive boss."
- **Abstract says:** "the association between JCS and subsequent career calling became more positive at higher levels of CSE across both time intervals, whereas CSE weakened the positive association between perceived inclusive leadership and subsequent career calling from T2 to T3"
- **Problem:** The CSE × inclusive leadership effect was observed only in the T2–T3 interval, not across the whole study; the digest presents it as a general pattern alongside the JCS effect, which did hold across both intervals.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier, e.g. '...but, in the second interval only, less additional benefit from an inclusive boss.'"

**Issue B. Prescription outruns the interaction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "note the signal that confident, self-assured employees may need autonomy more than inclusive encouragement, while less confident staff may need the reverse"
- **Abstract says:** "CSE weakened the positive association between perceived inclusive leadership and subsequent career calling from T2 to T3"
- **Problem:** A single weakening interaction in one time interval of a self-report longitudinal survey is turned into a differentiated management rule about who 'needs' what; the abstract reports associations, not needs.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as an untested implication: 'one interaction in the data hints that high-CSE employees may draw less additional calling from inclusive leadership — worth probing, not acting on.'"

### Study 3: Career exploration paid off for male students — and did nothing for the women
**Work ID:** W7226022739 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Which pathways gender moderated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "All four behaviors carried significant indirect effects, but gender moderated three of the four pathways, all stronger for men."
- **Abstract says:** "Gender moderated the associations between adaptability and crystallizing, deciding, and preparing, which were stronger for men. Exploring was linked to commitment for men but not for women. The indirect association through exploring differed significantly by gender."
- **Problem:** The three moderated effects were the adaptability-to-behavior links, not the indirect pathways; the indirect association that differed significantly by gender was the one through exploring — effectively the reverse of the digest's grouping.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say gender moderated the adaptability-to-crystallizing, -deciding and -preparing links (stronger for men), while the indirect path through exploring was the one that differed significantly by gender."

**Issue B. Open-ended responses over-attributed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "open-ended responses pointed to structural hurdles and family pressures constraining women's options"
- **Abstract says:** "Open-ended responses on students' hurdles and pressures in pursuing their preferred careers provided context for the findings."
- **Problem:** The abstract says the open-ended data provided context for all students' hurdles and pressures; it does not state they identified structural hurdles and family pressures specific to women.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'open-ended responses about students' hurdles and pressures in pursuing preferred careers provided context for the gender pattern.'"

**Issue C. Causal headline for correlational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Career exploration paid off for male students — and did nothing for the women"
- **Abstract says:** "Exploring was linked to commitment for men but not for women."
- **Problem:** 'Paid off' and 'did nothing' imply causal effects; the study reports associations across three waves, which the digest's own caveat acknowledges is correlational.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'Career exploration tracked with commitment for male students — but not for the women.'"

### Study 5: The students who felt most in control of their futures were the likeliest to take a gap year
**Work ID:** W7225739101 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intention stated as behavior in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The students who felt most in control of their futures were the likeliest to take a gap year"
- **Abstract says:** "In the final year of the academic program, higher career control was associated with a greater likelihood of planning an intentional gap year."
- **Problem:** The outcome was intended gap-year planning reported at one timepoint, not an actual gap year taken; the headline contradicts the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to '...were the likeliest to be planning a gap year.'"
