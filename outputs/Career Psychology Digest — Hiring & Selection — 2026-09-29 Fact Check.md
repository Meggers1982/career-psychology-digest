### Study 1: Hiring filters cut the applicant pool — and screen out the best candidates too
**Work ID:** W7203733318 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Theorized mechanism stated as finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "job offers were 8.9% more likely to be rejected and contracted wages were 2.5% higher, because everyone was chasing candidates with the same easily observable signals."
- **Abstract says:** "Additionally, I theorize that if many firms target the same signals of quality, they may all pursue similar candidates. This increases bargaining power for those applicants, leading to more rejected job offers and higher wage demands."
- **Problem:** The competition-for-the-same-candidates mechanism is theorized in the abstract, not demonstrated; the digest presents it as the established cause of the offer-stage results, which are themselves described as suggestive.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "which the author attributes to many employers chasing candidates with the same easily observable signals"."

### Study 4: Rejected by an algorithm? Adding a human reviewer changes how candidates feel about you
**Work ID:** W7204209860 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Proposition reported as finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Applicants rejected by AI tended to attribute responsibility to others, fueling hostility toward the employer"
- **Abstract says:** "Drawing on appraisal theory, we propose that applicants rejected by AI tend to appraise the rejection as others' responsibility, leading to negative emotions and adverse reactions against the organization."
- **Problem:** The abstract frames the other-blame appraisal as the theoretical proposition; only the profile shift from human review is described as a finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "The authors argue that AI rejection leads applicants to blame the organization rather than themselves..."."

**Issue B. Unlabelled gloss on emotion profiles** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "outward-focused negative emotions (anger at the organization) and inward-focused ones (self-blame, shame)"
- **Abstract says:** "we identify four distinct profiles based on the combination of outward-focused negative emotions (OFNE) and inward-focused negative emotions (IFNE)"
- **Problem:** The specific emotions named in parentheses are the digest's interpretation; the abstract does not specify which emotions were measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the parenthetical examples or mark them as illustrative."

### Study 8: Applicants have had ChatGPT for years. Pre-employment test scores barely budged
**Work ID:** W7202071941 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

### Study 9: Helping the new hire only pays off for you if they're good at the job
**Work ID:** W7202177851 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation chain flattened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When expectations were met, coworkers rated the newcomer as more competent, reported less stress in interacting with them, and were more willing to accept them as a legitimate member of the team."
- **Abstract says:** "met relationship expectations positively affect coworkers' evaluations of newcomers' work competence, which are then associated with lower levels of coworkers' stress when interacting with newcomers and higher levels of coworkers' acceptance of newcomers as legitimate organizational members"
- **Problem:** In the abstract, competence evaluations mediate the stress and acceptance outcomes; the digest lists all three as parallel effects of met expectations.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that met expectations raised competence evaluations, which in turn were linked to lower stress and greater acceptance."

**Issue B. Causal phrasing in takeaway** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in fact an unreciprocated helping relationship leaves the helper stressed and the newcomer less accepted"
- **Abstract says:** "may influence coworkers' evaluations... which are then associated with lower levels of coworkers' stress"
- **Problem:** The abstract uses hedged and associational language ("may influence", "associated with"); the digest states the effect as established fact.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "an unreciprocated helping relationship is linked to more stress for the helper and less acceptance of the newcomer"."

### Study 11: Employees without children are quietly keeping score on your flexibility policies
**Work ID:** W7213900915 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Within-group variation dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Parents valued flexibility when it came with managerial backing but some reported career penalties; employees childless by choice perceived inequity when parent-focused arrangements created extra availability demands on them without recognition"
- **Abstract says:** "Fairness perceptions varied both across and within family-status groups. ... Employees without children by choice perceived inequity when parent-centred arrangements generated additional availability expectations without corresponding recognition; more inclusive configurations were associated with favourable evaluations."
- **Problem:** The digest presents each family-status group as holding a single uniform view, whereas the abstract explicitly reports variation within groups and a positive configuration for the childless-by-choice group under inclusive arrangements. The paper's stated conclusion is that family status does not uniformly determine fairness judgments.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as "views varied within groups too — childless-by-choice employees judged more inclusive arrangements favourably"."

### Study 13: A two-minute allocation game predicts which job perks candidates actually care about
**Work ID:** W7204088664 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Invented task duration** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A two-minute allocation game predicts which job perks candidates actually care about"
- **Abstract says:** "Social Value Orientation (SVO), assessed via a behavioral resource-allocation instrument (i.e., the SVO slider measure)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no administration time for the SVO Slider, so "two-minute" is a specific detail not supported by the source.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the duration: "A short allocation game predicts which job perks candidates say they care about"."

**Issue B. Null result read as universal appeal** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "perks that benefit employees directly attract everyone regardless of values"
- **Abstract says:** "showing no significant association with employee CSR, consistent with the direct personal benefits it confers on employees"
- **Problem:** A non-significant association between SVO and employee-CSR importance means prosocial values did not predict that preference; it does not establish that employee-focused CSR "attracts everyone."
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "employee-focused CSR was valued similarly regardless of participants' prosocial values.""

### Study 14: Free tuition beats reimbursement: how education perks keep frontline workers from quitting
**Work ID:** W7211872890 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for correlational/intentions evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Free tuition beats reimbursement: how education perks keep frontline workers from quitting"
- **Abstract says:** "program participation is associated with reduced turnover ... educational benefits with little to no upfront costs—termed tuition assistance programs—are more strongly associated with reduced turnover intentions"
- **Problem:** The headline asserts that the benefit keeps workers from quitting, while Study 1 is an association and the design comparison rests on vignette-measured turnover intentions. The body and caveats hedge correctly, so only the headline overreaches. "Free tuition" also overstates "little to no upfront costs."
- **Suggested fix:** "Try "Up-front tuition support, not reimbursement: the education perk linked to lower quit intentions"."

### Study 18: What employees with ADHD say actually helps at work: informal relationships and flexible bosses
**Work ID:** W7208704204 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparative claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The relational environment, more than the work itself, shaped whether participants felt able to use their strengths."
- **Abstract says:** "this study examines how work relationships (i.e., socio-structural factors) enhance or hinder self-efficacy and goal attainment"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that work relationships facilitate or hinder thriving; it makes no comparison against the nature of the work itself. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the comparison: "Participants described the relational environment as decisive in whether they could use their strengths.""

**Issue B. Retention claim contradicted by own caveat** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "these workers point to everyday relational conditions — flexibility and approachable managers — as what determines whether they thrive or leave"
- **Abstract says:** "it examines neurodivergent employees' lived experiences of workplace relationships and investigates how these relationships facilitate or hinder their ability to thrive at work"
- **Problem:** The study measured no turnover outcome; the digest's caveat states the findings "cannot establish effects on performance or retention," so the body text contradicts the caveat by presenting relational conditions as determining whether people leave.
- **Suggested fix:** "Cut "or leave" and keep the claim to thriving at work as described by participants."
