### Study 2: Letting frontline staff redesign their own jobs cut burnout — by cutting demands, not by adding support
**Work ID:** W7205707686 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal wording for a non-randomized intervention** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Letting frontline staff redesign their own jobs cut burnout — by cutting demands, not by adding support"
- **Abstract says:** "The intervention was associated with lower job demands, higher co-worker instrumental support, and lower burnout disengagement. Multilevel analyses revealed that changes in job demands, rather than changes in support, appeared to account for the changes in burnout disengagement."
- **Problem:** The abstract consistently hedges ("associated with", "appeared to account for") because the groups were not randomized; the headline and the claim that redesign "can measurably reduce burnout" assert causation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "was linked to lower burnout" or "was associated with lower disengagement burnout, apparently via reduced demands"."

### Study 4: Bullying at work predicts burnout a year later — and psychological capital protects your life, not your job
**Work ID:** W7211962843 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported one-year lag in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Bullying at work predicts burnout a year later"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a two-wave study among a sample of 696 employees in Canada ... The results indicated that workplace bullying significantly predicted subsequent burnout."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no time interval between waves, so "a year later" is not supported (the digest's own caveats note the lag is unspecified, contradicting the headline).
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "predicts burnout at a later time point" or "predicts subsequent burnout"."

**Issue B. Direction of moderation inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Supervisor support and psychological capital (hope, optimism, resilience, self-efficacy) moderated bullying's effect on life satisfaction — but did not appear to shield people from burnout itself."
- **Abstract says:** "interaction effects suggested that psychological capital and supervisor support moderated the relationship between workplace bullying and life satisfaction"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a moderation but not that it was protective/buffering in form; it also does not report a tested-and-null moderation for burnout, so "did not appear to shield" is an inference from silence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the resources moderated the bullying–life-satisfaction link (buffering direction implied but not stated) and that no comparable moderation for burnout is reported in the abstract."

### Study 5: A five-day self-training course got employees to nudge themselves into better work habits
**Work ID:** W7213538292 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested comparison between prompts and intentions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The simplest trick here is environmental: leaving physical or digital prompts for yourself appears to do more to change work habits than intending to change does."
- **Abstract says:** "Process analyses using bootstrapping indicated that changes in self-nudging mediated the effect of the intervention on the sustainability outcomes within the present multi-component intervention design. ... The intervention also positively influenced sustainability intentions and sustainability behavior"
- **Problem:** The study found self-nudging mediated effects within a multi-component package; it did not pit prompts against intentions, and intentions also increased.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "changes in self-nudging appeared to carry much of the intervention's effect" without claiming prompts beat intentions."

### Study 6: Why your evening feels flat: unfinished tasks don't just nag, they sap your motivation to enjoy free time
**Work ID:** W7213645268 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unmeasured behavioral claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "which explains why evenings dissolve into passive scrolling"
- **Abstract says:** "evening experiential quality of leisure time (i.e. the extent to which free time is pleasurable, meaningful, and engaging)"
- **Problem:** The study measured experiential quality of leisure, not what people actually did with their evenings; passive scrolling was not assessed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the scrolling example or mark it explicitly as illustration, e.g. "which may help explain why free time feels unrewarding"."

### Study 7: Leaderboards at work: the competition feature in gamified apps predicts work addiction, burnout and quitting
**Work ID:** W7171963754 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Turnover intention framed as quitting** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the competition feature in gamified apps predicts work addiction, burnout and quitting"
- **Abstract says:** "leading to burnout ... and turnover intention (to protect oneself)"
- **Problem:** Intentions to leave are not actual quitting; no turnover behavior was measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "quitting" to "intentions to quit"."

**Issue B. Comparative claim about other affordances** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reward and status elements looked less damaging than head-to-head competition"
- **Abstract says:** "The results of our 3‐wave study ( N = 345 app‐workers) indicated that competition was associated with all outcomes as predicted."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that competition behaved as predicted; it does not report results for reward, status or self-expression, so a direct ranking of harm is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say only competition was linked to all the hypothesized outcomes, and that results for the other affordances are not reported in the abstract."

### Study 8: When family interrupts remote work, women with rigid schedules pay the biggest emotional price
**Work ID:** W7204753044 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb for diary-study moderation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It identifies a concrete, controllable lever -- schedule autonomy -- that determines whether a child walking into the home office is a minor blip or a route to evening exhaustion"
- **Abstract says:** "We find that the positive effect of daily family‐to‐work interruptions on end‐of‐workday negative affect is stronger for individuals with ... lower schedule autonomy."
- **Problem:** Schedule autonomy was a measured cross-level moderator in an observational diary study, not a manipulated lever shown to determine outcomes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "points to schedule autonomy as a factor associated with whether interruptions carry into evening exhaustion"."

### Study 9: A highly proactive boss with a less proactive team is a recipe for overload
**Work ID:** W7172021448 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of performance effect asserted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it manufactures demands the team can't absorb, and performance suffers through the overload channel"
- **Abstract says:** "team work overload mediates the relationship between proactive personality (in)congruence and team performance"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports mediation but does not state the sign of the incongruence–performance relationship; "performance suffers" and "manufactures demands" also add causal force to a correlational field design.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say overload mediated the link between (in)congruence and team performance, without specifying a magnitude or asserting causation."

### Study 10: Gamified gig platforms can reduce burnout -- but only when the algorithm watching you is seen as fair
**Work ID:** W7172490991 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline oversimplifies two separate moderators** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Gamified gig platforms can reduce burnout -- but only when the algorithm watching you is seen as fair"
- **Abstract says:** "Under low algorithmic monitoring, work gamification reduces surface emotional labor and thereby lowers burnout. Under high monitoring, this indirect effect weakens. Under high algorithmic fairness, gamification promotes deep emotional labor and thereby reduces burnout; under low fairness, the deep emotional labor pathway disappears."
- **Problem:** One burnout-reducing pathway depends on low monitoring, not fairness; the headline implies fairness is the single necessary condition.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "...but only when monitoring is light and the algorithm is seen as fair", matching the two pathways."

### Study 11: Stressed managers make for employees who log on while sick
**Work ID:** W7204491528 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Stressed managers make for employees who log on while sick"
- **Abstract says:** "Although findings should be interpreted cautiously given the cross-sectional design, they highlight managers as potential cross-level actors in shaping VP"
- **Problem:** "Make for" asserts that managerial strain produces employee presenteeism, while the abstract explicitly cautions that the design is cross-sectional and reports only associations. The digest's own caveat says causal direction cannot be established, so the headline contradicts it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to an association, e.g. "Where managers are under strain, employees are more likely to log on while sick"."

**Issue B. Direction of managerial working-conditions effect stated more precisely than the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Managers with less decision authority, colleague support and recognition reported more psychological distress"
- **Abstract says:** "Results showed that decision authority, colleague support, and recognition were associated with managers' psychological distress, which was negatively related to self-and employee-rated SMCs."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an association without specifying the sign for the resources-distress link; the digest states the inverse direction as established. It is a plausible reading but not verifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "managers' decision authority, colleague support and recognition were associated with their psychological distress" or note the direction as implied rather than stated."

### Study 12: It's not the age gap with your boss that matters -- it's whether either of you notices it
**Work ID:** W7172442198 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparative claim about objective age gap** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the number of years between a manager and a report matters far less than whether that gap is made conspicuous in day-to-day interaction"
- **Abstract says:** "Prior research on age differences in leader-follower dyads has primarily focused on objective age differences while giving less attention to whether such differences are psychologically salient... This study advances research... by shifting the focus from objective age differences to the salience of age differences"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports findings only for salience; it does not report a test showing that objective age differences are unrelated to LMX, engagement or exhaustion. The digest presents a head-to-head comparison that the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "what the study measured was salience, not the objective gap" rather than claiming the objective gap matters less."

**Issue B. "Either of you" implies dyadic data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it's whether either of you notices it"
- **Abstract says:** "we conducted a time-lagged survey with two measurement points four weeks apart, using a sample of 504 employees"
- **Problem:** Data came from employees only, so salience was assessed from the follower side; the headline implies leaders' perceptions were also measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "whether you notice it" or "whether the gap feels salient to the employee"."

**Issue C. Study description implies both constructs were measured and contrasted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "distinguished objective age differences between leaders and followers from the psychological salience of those differences"
- **Abstract says:** "This study examines how the salience of age differences between leaders and followers is associated with leader-member exchange (LMX) and, in turn, employees' work engagement and emotional exhaustion."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the conceptual shift to salience but does not state that objective age differences were modelled alongside it, so this is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that the study focused on the psychological salience of age differences, noting that the abstract does not report results for objective age gaps."
