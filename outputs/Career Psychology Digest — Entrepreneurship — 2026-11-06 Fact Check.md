### Study 2: There is no single recipe for passion at work — and entrepreneurs' version runs on different ingredients than employees'
**Work ID:** W7225316879 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal wording for configurational analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They identified multiple distinct combinations of conditions that each led to passion"
- **Abstract says:** "we identify multiple distinct configurations that not only differentiate harmonious from obsessive passion but also distinguish passion across the work and entrepreneurial domains"
- **Problem:** fsQCA identifies configurations associated with an outcome; "led to" implies causation, which the digest's own caveat explicitly rules out, so body and caveat contradict each other.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "multiple distinct combinations of conditions each associated with passion"."

**Issue B. Causal wording about obsessive passion pathways** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "including routes that produce unhealthy, obsessive passion"
- **Abstract says:** "we comparatively analyze configurations associated with harmonious and obsessive passion"
- **Problem:** "Produce" asserts a causal effect the design cannot establish.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "including configurations associated with obsessive passion"."

**Issue C. Practice implication not supported by the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "generic engagement programs that push the same levers for everyone are likely to miss most people"
- **Abstract says:** "Our study advances passion research by shifting attention from isolated net effects toward a holistic view of passion as a system of interdependent BPS predictors."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no intervention or engagement-program test; the prediction about program effectiveness goes beyond the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as a hypothesis, e.g. "this raises the question of whether one-size-fits-all engagement programs are well matched to how passion actually forms.""
