### Study 1: Rudeness from the boss hurts differently than rudeness from a peer
**Work ID:** W7225859305 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Coworker threat profile specified beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Coworker incivility produced a narrower threat profile, pointing more toward belonging."
- **Abstract says:** "Results show that the source of incivility shapes the nature and severity of psychological need threats. Supervisor incivility has a broader impact than coworker incivility, primarily reducing job satisfaction through control and self-esteem frustration. ... identifying distinct threat profiles for supervisor versus coworker incivility."
- **Problem:** The abstract confirms distinct profiles and a narrower impact for coworker incivility but never names belonging as the need coworker incivility threatens. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say coworker incivility produced a narrower threat profile, without naming which need, or attribute the belonging detail to the full paper."

**Issue B. Implied null effect for peer incivility** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "supervisor slights hit employees' sense of control and self-worth in a way peer slights do not"
- **Abstract says:** "Supervisor incivility has a broader impact than coworker incivility, primarily reducing job satisfaction through control and self-esteem frustration."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a broader impact for supervisor incivility, not an absence of control/self-esteem effects for coworker incivility; the digest asserts a null the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'hit employees' sense of control and self-worth more broadly than peer slights do.'"

### Study 2: Managers say work-related social media quietly rewrote the rules on being reachable
**Work ID:** W7224150475 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific resources not named in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The same platforms that supplied useful resources — contacts, visibility, information — also generated demands that eroded the ability to be off duty."
- **Abstract says:** "we highlight the impact of work-oriented SN on job demands, resources, work-life boundaries, and well-being"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to resources and demands generically; the specific resources (contacts, visibility, information) are the digest's elaboration. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the itemized examples or mark them as illustrative rather than findings."

### Study 3: Repetitive tasks may be the hidden hub that keeps job worries running after hours
**Work ID:** W7224080358 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Measured variables listed imprecisely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "measuring job demands, job insecurity, perceived employability and work-related rumination"
- **Abstract says:** "examine the longitudinal relationships among job stress, job insecurity, perceived employability, and work-related rumination ... task repetitiveness shows the strongest bridging capacity"
- **Problem:** The abstract frames the constructs as job stress (with task repetitiveness and job demands as components in the network); the digest's list omits task repetitiveness, the variable the story is built on, and substitutes 'job demands' for 'job stress'.
- **Suggested fix:** "List the constructs as job stress (including task repetitiveness and job demands), job insecurity, perceived employability and work-related rumination."

**Issue B. Bridging position treated as relative importance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the content of your job — how repetitive it is — may matter more than your workload or your willpower"
- **Abstract says:** "task repetitiveness shows the strongest bridging capacity and information diffusion function ... Job demands, job insecurity, and affective rumination jointly form the central structure of the system"
- **Problem:** Strongest bridging capacity means repetitiveness connects subsystems, not that it matters more than workload; job demands are themselves part of the central structure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'how repetitive your job is may be more tightly linked to after-hours brooding than you would expect', avoiding a ranking against workload."
