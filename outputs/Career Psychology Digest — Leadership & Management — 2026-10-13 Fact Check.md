### Study 1: Leadership isn't a personality — it's a string of small wins, and you can engineer them
**Work ID:** W7212753349 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Rating sources mischaracterized** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and it uses direct-report ratings rather than leaders' own flattering self-assessments"
- **Abstract says:** "subsequent transformational action (as rated by the self, others, and multiple direct reports)"
- **Problem:** The studies used self-ratings alongside other-ratings and direct-report ratings, not direct reports instead of self-ratings. The digest's own body text says as much, so the "Why it matters" line contradicts it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "and it supplements leaders' self-ratings with observer and multiple direct-report ratings.""

**Issue B. Mediation result stated more precisely than the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these micro-interventions raised leaders' momentary sense of psychological empowerment (meaning, competence, self-determination, impact), which in turn predicted more transformational action"
- **Abstract says:** "To test our theorizing that implicit leader prototype interventions trigger a state of psychological empowerment conducive to subsequent transformational action ... Taken together, our work provides a prescriptive and theoretically grounded means by which to cultivate transformational leadership"
- **Problem:** The abstract states the theorizing and an overall supportive conclusion but gives no effect sizes or explicit statement that empowerment mediated the effect; the specific causal chain is not verifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the chain to the authors' tested model ("the authors report support for a chain in which ...") and note that effect sizes are not given in the abstract."

### Study 2: Servant leaders get better results when they also deliver tough criticism
**Work ID:** W7221561375 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim about tough love without servant leadership** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Note that tough love only worked alongside genuine servant behavior; criticism without the supportive base is a different and riskier thing."
- **Abstract says:** "tough love is shown to moderate relations between servant leadership and both in-role performance and organizational citizenship behaviors"
- **Problem:** A moderation test shows the servant-leadership link is stronger at higher tough love; the abstract reports nothing about the effects of tough love delivered in the absence of servant leadership, so the "only worked alongside" claim is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the study tested tough love only as a moderator of servant leadership and did not examine criticism delivered without a supportive base."

**Issue B. Strength of moderation wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Tough love significantly strengthened the link between servant leadership and both in-role job performance and organizational citizenship behavior"
- **Abstract says:** "tough love is shown to moderate relations between servant leadership and both in-role performance and organizational citizenship behaviors"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports moderation consistent with the strengthening hypothesis but gives no statistics; "significantly strengthened" implies a reported significance test and magnitude not shown in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "moderated (in the hypothesized, strengthening direction)" and note that no effect sizes appear in the abstract."

### Study 3: When a manager plays favorites, the overlooked team member can still save the team
**Work ID:** W7220937301 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Proposed model reported as a result** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "They found that the proactivity of the least-supported member buffers the harm"
- **Abstract says:** "we propose and examine how the least-supported members’ proactivity mitigates the destructive effects of differentiated transformational leadership. We anticipate this research to bring important insights..."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes what the study proposes and examines and does not state that the buffering effect was supported; presenting it as a confirmed finding goes beyond the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write that the researchers proposed and tested whether least-supported members' proactivity buffers the harm, and flag that the abstract does not report the result (unverifiable from abstract only)."

### Study 4: An angry boss who can punish you makes you feel less than human — and you check out
**Work ID:** W7220922862 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Magnitude of moderation embellished** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "when the leader had little power to punish, the chain was much weaker"
- **Abstract says:** "This mediated relationship was weakened when the leader's coercive power was low."
- **Problem:** The abstract says the indirect effect was weakened, with no indication of how much; "much weaker" adds magnitude that cannot be checked.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "much" and say the mediated relationship was weaker when coercive power was low."

**Issue B. Number of studies** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across a set of studies including one with N=288"
- **Abstract says:** "= 288), we found that leader anger expression triggered followers' organizational dehumanization"
- **Problem:** The truncated abstract shows only one sample size and gives no information on how many studies were run; "a set of studies" is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say only that one reported sample had N = 288 and that the truncated abstract does not reveal the number of studies."

### Study 5: In restaurants, a manager's humility shows up as group trust — and then as service quality
**Work ID:** W7220913877 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome relabeled as service quality** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a manager's humility shows up as group trust — and then as service quality"
- **Abstract says:** "servant leadership cultivated group trust at the store level, which, in turn, positively impacted service climate"
- **Problem:** Service climate is employees' shared perception of the emphasis on service, not measured customer-facing service quality; the headline implies a customer outcome the study did not measure. The body text uses the correct term.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to "...and then as service climate" and avoid implying customers' experience was measured."

**Issue B. Implied customer experience** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "to the service climate customers experience"
- **Abstract says:** "servant leadership cultivated group trust at the store level, which, in turn, positively impacted service climate"
- **Problem:** Service climate was rated by employees; no customer data are reported in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "to the service climate employees report" and note that customer outcomes were not measured."

### Study 6: Delegation only lands well when the boss shares credit — and absorbs blame
**Work ID:** W7204196725 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Publicly" not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Leaders who publicly took the blame when delegated work went wrong"
- **Abstract says:** "leader blame taking consistently weakens the delegation–hindrance appraisal link, reducing turnover intention"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes leader blame taking without specifying that it must be public; the public element is an added detail.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "publicly" or mark it as an interpretation not specified in the abstract."

**Issue B. Credit-sharing effect attributed to both experiments** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "leaders who shared credit boosted challenge appraisals and proactive 'taking charge' in the experiments"
- **Abstract says:** "Leader credit sharing strengthens the delegation–challenge appraisal link and subsequent taking charge in the experiment, but this effect is not replicated in the field study."
- **Problem:** Only Study 1a tested the challenge pathway, so the effect comes from one experiment, not both.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "in the challenge-pathway experiment (Study 1a, N = 217).""

**Issue C. Moderation described as a main effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "consistently reduced employees' hindrance appraisals and turnover intentions"
- **Abstract says:** "leader blame taking consistently weakens the delegation–hindrance appraisal link, reducing turnover intention"
- **Problem:** Blame taking weakened the link between delegation and hindrance appraisal; the digest states it lowered hindrance appraisals outright.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "weakened the link between delegation and hindrance appraisal, and in turn turnover intention.""

### Study 7: High-performance HR can breed misbehavior in hospitals — unless leaders serve rather than drive
**Work ID:** W7214609077 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal framing of a correlational multilevel study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this suggests their hidden cost runs through broken promises and heavier workloads into how staff treat each other"
- **Abstract says:** "high-performance work systems (HPWS) may inadvertently correlate with adverse employee outcomes ... psychological contract breach and work intensification serve as mediators between HPWS and interpersonal deviance"
- **Problem:** The abstract is explicitly correlational ("may inadvertently correlate"); the body text asserts a causal pathway, which sits uneasily with the digest's own caveat that causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "these variables statistically mediated the association" and keep the correlational hedge in the body, not just the caveats."

**Issue B. "Sabotage" overstates organizational deviance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "stops colleague-to-colleague friction from becoming sabotage of the organization"
- **Abstract says:** "the progression of interpersonal deviance to organizational deviance is moderated by servant leadership, which diminishes the upward trajectory of deviant actions inside the organization"
- **Problem:** Organizational deviance covers a range of counterproductive behaviors; "sabotage" implies a severe, specific act the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "organization-directed deviance" instead of "sabotage.""

**Issue C. Design details not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cross-sectional self-report design, so causation cannot be established"
- **Abstract says:** "The current multilevel research is based on data from 345 medical staff nested within 226 hospitals in healthcare organizations (hospitals)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state the time structure or the rating source; the cross-sectional and self-report labels are inferences.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the abstract does not specify the time design or data sources (unverifiable from abstract only)."
