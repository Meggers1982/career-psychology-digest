### Study 2: Standard personality tests may quietly screen out autistic job candidates
**Work ID:** W7207655302 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of trait differences not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "autistic respondents scored substantially lower on extraversion and conscientiousness"
- **Abstract says:** "not due to measurement noninvariance, but because of substantial mean trait differences likely leading to adverse impact, particularly with the traits of extraversion and conscientiousness"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports substantial mean differences on those two traits but never specifies that autistic respondents scored lower. The direction is plausible but unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "autistic respondents differed substantially on extraversion and conscientiousness (in a direction the authors say is likely to produce adverse impact)" or cite the full text for the direction."

**Issue B. Added claim about employer screening practice** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the two traits employers most often screen on"
- **Abstract says:** "there is a need to evaluate how they are used in selection contexts to ensure fairness for autistic individuals"
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim about which traits employers screen on most often; this is the digest's own assertion presented as part of the study's finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mark it as context ("two traits commonly used in selection") rather than a study finding."

### Study 3: Impact investors say they fund the neediest places — the data says they mostly fund nearby ones
**Work ID:** W7211933722 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Theoretical mechanism presented as tested finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Geographic distance significantly reduced the likelihood that a deal actually closed, because distance raises the costs of screening, contracting, monitoring, and supporting ventures."
- **Abstract says:** "we argue that impact investment decisions reflect the joint consideration of anticipated transaction value and governance costs. Distance raises the costs of screening, contracting, monitoring, and supporting ventures ... Distance reduces the likelihood of deal realisation."
- **Problem:** Governance costs are the authors' theorized explanation, not a variable shown to mediate the distance effect in the analysis reported in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the authors interpret the distance penalty as reflecting screening, contracting and monitoring costs."

**Issue B. Headline attributes a claim to investors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Impact investors say they fund the neediest places"
- **Abstract says:** "Impact investing is widely expected to direct capital to locations where social and environmental challenges are most severe."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a general expectation of the field, not statements by investors themselves.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "Impact investing is supposed to fund the neediest places — the data says it mostly funds nearby ones.""

### Study 9: Leadership experience only builds a leader identity in people who remember the experience well
**Work ID:** W7213545411 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested intervention implied as the lever** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "points at reflection and structured debriefs as the lever that closes the gap"
- **Abstract says:** "We derive implications for event system theory and the practical applications of EBC for leadership self-concept formation."
- **Problem:** Event binding capacity was measured as an individual difference and moderator; nothing in the abstract tests reflection, debriefs or any intervention that would raise or compensate for it, so presenting it as "the lever" overstates the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame reflection and debriefs as an untested speculation the authors' findings might motivate, not as something the study points to as effective."

**Issue B. Study 1 null result omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In Study 1, prior leadership experience predicted a stronger leader identity only among people with higher event binding capacity."
- **Abstract says:** "In study 1, findings revealed that EBC moderated the relationship between prior leadership experience on leader identity, but not on leadership self-efficacy."
- **Problem:** The moderation did not hold for leadership self-efficacy, a boundary the digest leaves out.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the same moderation did not appear for leadership self-efficacy in Study 1."

### Study 10: When work gets boring, the damage shows up the same week — unless you have control over your job
**Work ID:** W7204556045 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Country of researchers/sample not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dutch researchers ran a three-wave survey six weeks apart with working adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Survey data were collected in a three-wave design with six weeks between waves (nt1 = 1018; nt2 = 750; nt3 = 547)."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no country for the researchers or the sample, so both "Dutch" and the "single-country sample" caveat are unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the nationality or confirm it from the full text."

**Issue B. Moderation scope slightly broadened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "they were weaker for employees who had more control over how they did their work"
- **Abstract says:** "changes in work-related boredom were positively associated with concurrent (but not lagged) changes in depressed mood and frustration, and that these relationships were weaker for higher levels of job control"
- **Problem:** Job control is reported as moderating the boredom-to-affect links; the digest's "they" reads as if it buffered the whole chain through to procrastination and counterproductive behavior.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that job control weakened the link between rising boredom and depressed mood/frustration."

### Study 11: Reject a good idea and people stop trusting you — including the ones just watching
**Work ID:** W7159739744 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unverifiable study-design details in Caveats** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two controlled experiments with hypothetical idea-evaluation scenarios rather than real organizational decisions; ... trust was measured immediately, so nothing is known about durability"
- **Abstract says:** "We theorize and show in two experiments that idea generators and third-party observers trust a decision-maker less when the decision-maker fails to recognize a creative idea"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe the scenarios as hypothetical, nor state when trust was measured; these are plausible but unverified assumptions presented as fact.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the abstract does not describe the experimental scenarios or the timing of the trust measure" or mark as unverifiable from abstract only."

**Issue B. Omitted parallel mediator** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the reason was competence: the rejecter looked less capable"
- **Abstract says:** "We also find perceived warmth as a parallel mediator, but only when using participants' subjective creativity perceptions."
- **Problem:** Competence is presented as the sole mechanism; the abstract also reports perceived warmth as a parallel mediator under one operationalization.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that perceived warmth also mediated the effect when subjective creativity perceptions were used."

### Study 12: Company-sponsored expatriates react to career shocks more actively than those who moved abroad on their own

- **Abstract says:** "tracking 26 expatriates in Australia over four years (n = 52 interviews)"

### Study 14: Bullying at work comes in bundles — and the worst mix drives drinking and quitting
**Work ID:** W7207619744 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Two profile solutions merged** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Four profiles emerged, ranging from low to high exposure, including distinct patterns dominated by social bullying versus verbal-plus-physical bullying."
- **Abstract says:** "Our results revealed four profiles characterised by Low, Moderately Low, Moderately High, and High levels of bullying behaviour. Alternative results from a bifactor measurement model also revealed four profiles: High Global and Physical Bullying Behavior, High Global and Social Bullying Behavior, Normative, and High Global, Verbal, and Physical Bullying Behavior."
- **Problem:** These are two separate four-profile solutions (a severity-graded one and an alternative bifactor one), not a single set containing both severity levels and behavior-specific patterns.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the main solution graded severity from Low to High, while an alternative bifactor model produced behaviour-specific profiles."

**Issue B. Predictive/temporal framing of correlates** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Profile membership was predicted by role ambiguity, role conflict and perceived organizational support ... the study also points to role clarity and visible organizational support as the structural fixes that precede profile membership"
- **Abstract says:** "Role ambiguity, role conflict, and perceived organisational support were all associated with profile membership."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports association only; "predicted by" and especially "precede profile membership" imply temporal ordering and causal leverage the abstract does not claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "were associated with profile membership" and drop "precede"."

### Study 15: Why some critical feedback lands and some flattens you: it depends on how surprising and disruptive it is
**Work ID:** W7207697928 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mechanisms described as competing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They tested two competing pathways: feedback that reduced felt competence versus feedback that drained cognitive resources."
- **Abstract says:** "...relate to employee effectiveness... via two mechanisms—felt competence and cognitive depletion"
- **Problem:** The abstract presents felt competence and cognitive depletion as two complementary mediators in one model, not as rival explanations pitted against each other.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe them as two mediating mechanisms tested in the same model."

**Issue B. Unsupported content-versus-form comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the content matters less than whether the feedback arrives as a surprise, interrupts ongoing work, and feels consequential"
- **Abstract says:** "specific, event-based characteristics of negative feedback play an important role in explaining employee reactions to such feedback"
- **Problem:** The study did not compare feedback content against event characteristics, so it cannot support a claim that content matters less.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say event characteristics matter in addition to content, rather than more than it."

### Study 16: Job insecurity doesn't just stress people out — it makes them hide and scroll
**Work ID:** W7204954329 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verbs for a time-lagged correlational survey** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Insecurity raised workplace anxiety, which in turn increased cyberloafing and decreased help-seeking"
- **Abstract says:** "A three-wave, one-month time-lagged survey was conducted among 321 full-time employees in China... Workplace anxiety mediated the relationships between job insecurity and the two coping responses"
- **Problem:** The body text asserts causal effects that the self-report, time-lagged design cannot establish, partly contradicting the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use associational wording: "insecurity was associated with higher anxiety, which in turn related to more cyberloafing and less help-seeking.""

**Issue B. Moderator simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those with a strong prevention focus cyberloafed more"
- **Abstract says:** "prevention focus amplified the positive association between anxiety and cyberloafing"
- **Problem:** Prevention focus strengthened the anxiety–cyberloafing link rather than producing a main effect on cyberloafing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "prevention-focused employees showed a stronger link between anxiety and cyberloafing.""

### Study 17: How companies learn to hire from places they never looked: lessons from 25 years of baseball scouting
**Work ID:** W7204877748 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported relative weighting of drivers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "mostly by copying rivals and responding to visible evidence, rather than by independent analysis"
- **Abstract says:** "Results indicate mimetic and coercive institutional pressures, top manager institutional agency, and field-level performance feedback systematically influence adoption decisions."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that several pressures systematically influenced adoption but gives no ranking, so "mostly" overstates what is known about relative importance.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say adoption was shaped by rivals' moves, demand conditions, managerial agency and visible performance feedback, without ranking them."

### Study 18: An inspiring vision only works on employees who already love the job
**Work ID:** W7204870087 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal and magnitude language** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Vision communication improved performance only indirectly — by making the vision seem plausible — and that link was much stronger among followers high in harmonious work passion"
- **Abstract says:** "follower harmonious work passion amplifies the impact of leader vision communication on perceived vision plausibility, which mediates the relationship between leader vision communication and follower job performance"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports mediation in a time-lagged survey, not a causal improvement, says nothing about the absence of a direct effect ("only indirectly"), and gives no effect size to support "much stronger".
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "vision communication was linked to performance via perceived plausibility, and that link was stronger among followers high in harmonious passion.""

**Issue B. Caveat misdescribes the data sources** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "performance ratings come from leaders who also were rated on vision communication within the same dyad"
- **Abstract says:** "A multisource, time-lagged survey of 229 leader–follower dyads tested these hypothesized relationships."
- **Problem:** The abstract states the design was multisource but does not specify who rated what, so this specific common-source concern cannot be verified and may misstate the design.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the design was multisource and time-lagged, and that the abstract does not specify which rater supplied each measure."
