### Study 1: When a machine screens you: job applicants in Chile, Germany and India react very differently
**Work ID:** W7223109819 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped null country result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Perceived consistency of the AI did not mediate anything, and country mattered: Chilean and Indian applicants rated AI as less consistent than German applicants did, while Germans saw it as more innovative than Indians did."
- **Abstract says:** "Moreover, German applicants perceived AI as more innovative than Indian applicants, while no significant country-level differences emerged for opportunity to perform."
- **Problem:** The headline and body emphasize that applicants in the three countries "react very differently," but the abstract reports no significant country-level differences on the key opportunity-to-perform perception. Omitting that null result makes cross-country variation look broader than it was.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as "though the sense of reduced opportunity to perform was similar across all three countries.""

**Issue B. Unsupported mechanism (institutional trust)** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and that this gut reaction is shaped partly by how much institutional trust exists in the country you are applying in"
- **Abstract says:** "Drawing on signaling theory and North's institutional perspective ... proposing that country-level differences in formal and informal factors shape applicants' perceptions"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers generically to country-level formal and informal institutional factors; it does not measure or name "institutional trust" as the mechanism, and the only cross-country differences found were for consistency and innovativeness, not the "gut reaction" of feeling unable to make one's case.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "shaped partly by the formal and informal institutional context of the country you are applying in," and tie it to the consistency/innovativeness differences the study actually found."

### Study 2: The evidence that hiring tests predict job performance is shakier than the textbooks say
**Work ID:** W7222969019 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Author count not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two selection researchers systematically reviewed the most recent meta-analytic literature"
- **Abstract says:** "We reviewed the most recent meta‐analytic literature on the criterion validity of predictors of job performance used in personnel selection."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state how many authors wrote the piece, nor does it describe the review as "systematic" in the formal methodological sense. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the author count and "systematically," e.g. "Selection researchers reviewed the most recent meta-analytic literature...""

**Issue B. Unsourced counterpoint in Caveats** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and other researchers in the field dispute the severity of these corrections"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract contains no mention of disagreement from other researchers; it notes only that the authors observed "increasing attention in meta-analyses to heterogeneity of results, publication bias and open science.""
- **Problem:** The existence and nature of a scholarly dispute cannot be verified from this abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either attribute the counterpoint to a named source or remove it; consider substituting the abstract's own positive note about improving meta-analytic practice."

**Issue C. Quantifier slightly stronger than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most underlying data come from concurrent studies of current employees"
- **Abstract says:** "The overreliance on concurrent incumbent studies leads to meta‐analytic estimates based on data collected in conditions with non‐representative stakes"
- **Problem:** "Overreliance" is a judgment about proportion and emphasis; the abstract does not quantify it as "most."
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "the field relies too heavily on concurrent studies of current employees.""

### Study 3: In Brazil, Black workers report that work meets fewer of their basic needs
**Work ID:** W7224595503 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Invariance stated more firmly than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The five-factor structure held up and measured equivalently across gender and race/color groups, meaning the comparisons are meaningful rather than artifacts of the instrument."
- **Abstract says:** "Invariance analyses indicated metric and scalar equivalence across gender and race/color based on practical fit-index criteria."
- **Problem:** The abstract qualifies the invariance conclusion as resting on practical fit-index criteria (i.e., change-in-fit thresholds rather than strict chi-square tests); the digest presents equivalence as unqualified.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "by practical fit-index standards" to the sentence about measurement equivalence."

**Issue B. "Workers" vs. adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In Brazil, Black workers report that work meets fewer of their basic needs"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were 1521 Brazilian adults (67.2% women; mean age = 35.09 years), recruited across three independent waves of data collection."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the sample as Brazilian adults recruited in three waves, not as a verified sample of employed workers or a representative labor-market sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "Black Brazilian adults surveyed" rather than "Black workers," or note in the body that participants were a convenience sample of adults."
