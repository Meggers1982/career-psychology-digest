### Study 1: Software that scores what applicants write — without a black box
**Work ID:** W7225331132 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Material type not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "applied to narrative application material such as open-ended responses and essays"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract (truncated) refers only to "passively scoring narrative application information" and "information already gathered"; it does not specify open-ended responses or essays."
- **Problem:** The specific examples of narrative material are not verifiable from the abstract as provided.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "narrative application information" or note that the specific material types come from the full paper."

**Issue B. Number of studies unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers assembled a large multi-study program (total N=67,652)"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract gives only "= 67,652), including employment and school settings, experimental and operational contexts, concurrent and predictive designs, and a range of occupations.""
- **Problem:** The abstract confirms the total N and the mix of settings but does not state that this was a multi-study program or how many studies were involved.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe it as evidence aggregated across dictionaries, settings and designs totalling N=67,652, without asserting a specific multi-study structure."

### Study 2: The quietly powerful hiring test most employers underuse
**Work ID:** W7225104062 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison to aptitude testing not in abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It suggests a well-built, job-specific knowledge test can rival or beat general aptitude testing"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports operational validities for JKTs (.34 job-specific vs .19 nonjob-specific) and that job knowledge fully mediated the cognitive ability–performance relationship; it does not report a validity figure for cognitive ability tests or compare the two."
- **Problem:** Readers may act on a head-to-head comparison the abstract never makes; the mediation finding is about mechanism, not relative validity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "job-specific knowledge tests showed substantially higher validity than generic ones, and job knowledge statistically accounted for cognitive ability's link to performance.""

**Issue B. Causal gloss on mediation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "general mental ability may predict performance largely because smart people acquire job knowledge faster"
- **Abstract says:** ""Job knowledge statistically fully mediated the relationship between cognitive ability and job performance.""
- **Problem:** Statistical mediation in correlational meta-analytic data does not establish that higher-ability people acquire knowledge faster; the hedge "may" softens but does not remove the causal story.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the mediation result is consistent with knowledge being the pathway, while noting the data are correlational."

### Study 5: Transgender applicants were rated less hireable — until the resume said more about them
**Work ID:** W7225306551 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Participant gender identity not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cisgender participants evaluated either a transgender woman or a cisgender woman applying for a nursing role"
- **Abstract says:** ""Using a between-participants in-basket experimental design with Indian professionals, we examined evaluations of a transgender woman and a cisgender woman applicant for a nursing role." The theoretical framing refers to "cisgender hiring managers," but the sample is described only as Indian professionals."
