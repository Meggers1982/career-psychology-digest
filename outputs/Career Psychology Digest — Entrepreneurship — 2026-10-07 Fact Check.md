### Study 1: Startups hit by a disaster in year one stay scrappier — and riskier — for years
**Work ID:** W7220410857 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Persistence horizon not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests that the conditions of a company's first year leave structural fingerprints that persist for a decade or more"
- **Abstract says:** "We study whether shocks from local natural disasters can leave durable organizational traces in young high-growth ventures... These durable organizational traces may, in turn, shape ventures' subsequent capacity for reconfiguration."
- **Problem:** The abstract says "durable" traces but never quantifies the duration; "a decade or more" is a specific claim that cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "leave durable structural fingerprints" or "traces that persist well into the firm's growth"."

**Issue B. Descriptive evidence stated more firmly than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Employee-level data suggested these firms retained flatter structures, more fluid roles and broader expertise than comparable peers as they grew."
- **Abstract says:** "Descriptive employee-level evidence further documents differences in organizational structure and composition consistent with greater retained fluidity among exposed ventures."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only differences in structure and composition "consistent with greater retained fluidity"; the specific list (flatness, role fluidity, broader expertise) comes from the authors' theoretical interpretation, not from what the employee data directly showed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Descriptive employee-level data showed structural differences consistent with greater retained fluidity, which the authors interpret as flatness, role fluidity and broader expertise.""

### Study 2: North Korean defectors who started businesses built three very different kinds of companies — and the difference was identity
**Work ID:** W7214760936 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Country of venture creation not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers followed 23 North Korean defector founders building ventures in South Korea"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a 27-month field study of 23 North Korean Defector (NKD) founders... how refugee founders respond to being in a new country"
- **Problem:** The abstract never names South Korea as the setting; it refers only to "a new country." Unverifiable from abstract only (though a plausible inference).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "building ventures in their new country" unless the setting is confirmed in the full text."

### Study 3: Why women founders post about their worst days — and how they manage the fallout
**Work ID:** W7215047180 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Gendered penalties framed as a documented finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "rather than assuming authenticity is risk-free, especially given the gendered penalties the study documents"
- **Abstract says:** "These disclosures expose entrepreneurs to interpersonal risks, heightened for women entrepreneurs who contend with gendered biases and less supportive social contexts."
- **Problem:** The abstract presents heightened risk for women as background motivation for the study's focus, not as a measured or documented outcome; the study reports no comparative test of penalties.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "given the gendered biases the study takes as its starting point" or "the heightened interpersonal risks the authors describe"."
