### Study 1: A short nudge changed how managers responded to a downturn — and the effect grew over three months
**Work ID:** W7202103093 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of the baseline effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Among untreated managers, when perceived threat rose they shifted toward a prevention focus and spoke up less about opportunities"
- **Abstract says:** "Among unexposed managers, within-person increases in perceived threat were associated with lower promotion focus and opportunity-related voice, the threat-bias pattern."
- **Problem:** The abstract documents a drop in promotion focus, not a rise in prevention focus; under regulatory focus theory these are independent systems, which is the paper's core claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: "when perceived threat rose, their promotion focus fell and they spoke up less about opportunities"."

**Issue B. "Broke that link" overstates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "both interventions broke that link and raised promotion focus and opportunity-related voice"
- **Abstract says:** "Both interventions weakened that association and raised promotion focus and opportunity-related voice relative to control"
- **Problem:** "Broke" implies the association was eliminated; the abstract says it was weakened.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "weakened that link"."

### Study 3: Women get asked for advice as often as men — but only if they're seen as highly prosocial
**Work ID:** W7214315853 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Preregistration not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "two preregistered-style online experiments (n=247 and n=248)"
- **Abstract says:** "Two online experiments (Study 2a, n = 247; Study 2b, n = 248) provide converging causal evidence for these effects."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim about preregistration; "preregistered-style" is an invented methodological descriptor that lends unearned rigor.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the modifier: "two online experiments (n=247 and n=248)"."

**Issue B. Rating source specified beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Advice centrality then mediated effects on leadership emergence and supervisor performance ratings"
- **Abstract says:** "Advice network centrality further mediates the interactive effects of gender and prosocial motivation on leadership emergence and performance ratings."
- **Problem:** The abstract says only "performance ratings" and does not identify the rater; attributing them to supervisors is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "performance ratings" without naming the rating source."

**Issue C. Motivation vs. perception in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but only if they're seen as highly prosocial"
- **Abstract says:** "women are less likely than men to occupy central positions in advice networks, but that high prosocial motivation closes this gap"
- **Problem:** The construct is the woman's own prosocial motivation, not colleagues' perception of her as prosocial.
- **Suggested fix:** ""— but only if they are highly prosocially motivated"."

### Study 9: The "identity pause": what perimenopausal workers did instead of pushing through
**Work ID:** W7220928682 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Career-stage claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Perimenopause affects most women, often at the peak career stage"
- **Abstract says:** "perimenopause, which is a naturally occurring physiological transition experienced by most women"
- **Problem:** "Most women" is supported; the "peak career stage" framing is an addition not present in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the career-stage clause or label it as context rather than a study finding."

**Issue B. Prescription outruns theory-building study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests the useful organizational response is permission to ease off temporarily rather than accommodations aimed at restoring full performance of the old self"
- **Abstract says:** "our findings point to the criticality of an identity pause, which involves taking respite from striving to enact idealized identities… advancing research on the body–identity nexus, embodiment, and women and work."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes an individual-level embodiment mechanism; it does not compare organizational responses or test the effectiveness of any accommodation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as a hypothesis the study raises for employers rather than what the evidence shows is "the useful organizational response"."

### Study 13: Harassed by a customer, with no one watching: what protects gig workers
**Work ID:** W7218644956 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediator pathways conflated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "workers reported higher psychological distress and a greater sense of threat to the self, and in turn cut corners on the work itself (production deviance) and behaved more rudely or hostilely toward customers (interpersonal deviance)"
- **Abstract says:** "the indirect detrimental effects of CSH on production deviance and interpersonal deviance, through psychological distress and self-threat, respectively, were weaker for gig workers who had access to collective representation"
- **Problem:** The abstract pairs distress specifically with production deviance and self-threat specifically with interpersonal deviance; the digest presents both mediators as leading to both outcomes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that distress fed production deviance while self-threat fed interpersonal deviance."

**Issue B. Unverified examples of collective representation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "workers who had access to some form of collective representation, such as a union, worker association or organized peer body"
- **Abstract says:** "gig workers who had access to collective representation than those who lacked such access"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify what forms of collective representation were measured; the illustrative list is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the examples or mark them as illustrative rather than as what was measured."

### Study 14: Money worries make employees angry — and the anger shows up in their work
**Work ID:** W7215113460 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal framing of time-lagged associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Money worries make employees angry — and the anger shows up in their work"
- **Abstract says:** "financial insecurity was indirectly associated with counterproductive work behaviour via anger … These findings identify anger as an affective pathway linking financial insecurity to detrimental work outcomes"
- **Problem:** The abstract consistently uses associational language for multi-wave correlational data; the headline and the body phrase "whether financial insecurity harms work behavior" assert causation. The caveats partly correct this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "is linked to" / "goes with" in the headline and body."

**Issue B. Task-performance result generalized to both studies** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In both, employees who felt their financial resources were inadequate or unstable reported more anger, and that anger predicted more counterproductive work behavior; evidence for an effect on task performance was weaker."
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 … showed that financial insecurity was indirectly associated with counterproductive work behaviour via anger, with weaker evidence for task performance. Study 2 … replicated the indirect association between financial insecurity and counterproductive work behaviour through anger."
- **Problem:** The weaker task-performance evidence is reported only for Study 1; the abstract does not say task performance was examined in Study 2.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the weaker task-performance finding to Study 1 specifically."

**Work ID:** W7212394179 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator stated as a necessary condition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Office politics can fuel creativity — but only where the boss plays favorites on merit"
