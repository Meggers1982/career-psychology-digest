### Study 1: A 23,000-person, 42-country test of "he or she" finds it doesn't budge the leader-equals-man stereotype
**Work ID:** W7171908932 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Largest test yet" not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the largest test yet suggesting that on its own it does not change who people picture as a leader"
- **Abstract says:** "This project suggests that the ability of gender-fair language interventions to ameliorate gender bias in the political leadership domain may be limited."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a large multi-country sample but never claims this is the largest test of gender-fair language. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "one of the largest tests to date" or drop the superlative."

**Issue B. Gloss on the community-stereotype gap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The study also shows people believe their community holds stronger male-leader stereotypes than they personally admit to"
- **Abstract says:** "participants stereotyped political leaders as more similar to men than women; this pattern was stronger on the incentivised measure of community stereotypes"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a stronger pattern on the incentivized community measure than on the self-report measure; whether that reflects what people "personally admit to" versus actually believe is an interpretation, not a reported finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the male-leader stereotype was stronger when people estimated their community's views than on the standard self-report measure."

### Study 2: Career tests that measure only interests push women away from STEM — adding four more dimensions narrows the gap
**Work ID:** W7214020374 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline from a cross-sectional assessment study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Career tests that measure only interests push women away from STEM"
- **Abstract says:** "career fit assessments based solely on vocational interests may reinforce gendered occupational segregation... interests, knowledge, and skills produced the largest gender gaps in career recommendations"
- **Problem:** The study compared gender gaps in recommendations generated by different assessment types in one cross-sectional sample; it did not track whether anyone was steered away from a STEM career. The abstract itself hedges with "may reinforce".
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Career tests built only on interests produce the widest gender gaps in STEM recommendations"."

**Issue B. "Substantially more" overstates the reported result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "substantially more men and women were recommended for STEM careers"
- **Abstract says:** "This resulted in larger percentages of both men and women recommended across STEM careers."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports "larger percentages" without quantifying magnitude; "substantially" adds emphasis the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "larger percentages of both men and women were recommended"."

### Study 7: "Alienating" or "a threat": what employees actually say about their company's diversity programs
**Work ID:** W7214634718 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Story angle adds unstated specifics** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "when programs are justified mainly by business benefit, participants read them as marketing, and defensiveness among majority-group staff hardens rather than softens"
- **Abstract says:** "Between the status groups, negatively biased mental representations, attribution and projection, externalization and defensiveness solidify the reverse outcomes... diversity management then settles into a unitarist logic that subordinates its normative objective to economic interest"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes defensiveness "between the status groups" generally and does not report that business-case framing specifically triggers it or that defensiveness is concentrated among majority-group staff.

### Study 8: Green nudges at work really do change behavior — but the biggest wins come from fixing the office, not lecturing staff
**Work ID:** W7213915259 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts a moderator ranking the abstract doesn't give** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the biggest wins come from fixing the office, not lecturing staff"
- **Abstract says:** "Moderation analyses revealed systematic differences in effect sizes, significance, prevalence, and heterogeneity."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that ability-, motivation- and opportunity-directed interventions differed systematically but never states that infrastructural (opportunity) interventions were the most effective. Readers would act on a ranking the abstract does not supply.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the three intervention types differed systematically in effectiveness and that the authors emphasize intervention design features over individual psychological constraints."

**Issue B. Budget recommendation overruns the evidence** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "suggests structural and infrastructural changes deserve more of the budget than awareness campaigns"
- **Abstract says:** "we challenge existing theoretical frameworks focusing on the individual-level psychological constraints and use the AMO framework to highlight the importance of design features of interventions"
- **Problem:** The abstract's claim is about design features mattering more than individual psychological constraints, not about infrastructure outperforming information/feedback interventions; the budget advice rests on a comparison not reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as: the evidence supports intervention design, not individual mindset, as the lever — with the specific ranking of methods not available from the abstract."

### Study 11: Men with power think the system is fair. Women with the same titles don't.
**Work ID:** W7203448124 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Perceived resources presented as actual resources** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Men's authority was far more likely than women's to come with "resource sufficiency": the budget, staff and instrumental support needed to actually exercise the role."
- **Abstract says:** "men's power is more often accompanied by perceived resource sufficiency , or the appraisal that one has adequate instrumental and material resources to meet role demands and exercise authority effectively"
- **Problem:** The construct is a self-appraisal, not a measure of real budget or headcount. The digest's wording (and the manager advice to "audit ... who holds which resources") implies the study documented objective resource gaps between men's and women's roles, which the abstract does not claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "men in powerful roles were more likely to *perceive* that they had the resources needed to exercise authority," and note the construct is an appraisal throughout, not just in Study 4."

**Issue B. Magnitude sharpened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "far more likely than women's"
- **Abstract says:** "men's power is more often accompanied by perceived resource sufficiency"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a difference without quantifying it; "far more likely" adds an effect size the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "more often" or "more likely" without the intensifier."

### Study 12: Deep roots in your community make motherhood discrimination harder to endure, not easier
**Work ID:** W7212085651 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Health cost of staying" misstates the mediation path** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "health costs show up for mothers who stay stuck in a discriminatory setting"
- **Abstract says:** "turnover intentions mediate the positive relationships between motherhood discrimination and both voluntary turnover and poor physical health. Moreover, high on-the-job embeddedness weakens the relationship between motherhood discrimination and turnover intentions, reducing its indirect effects on voluntary turnover and poor physical health."
- **Problem:** The modelled path runs discrimination → turnover intentions → poor physical health; the study did not compare the health of mothers who stayed with those who left, and high on-the-job embeddedness (a reason to stay) actually reduced the indirect health effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "discrimination was linked to poorer physical health via heightened intentions to quit," and drop the implication that staying per se causes the health cost."

### Study 14: How an Indian organization helped widowed women unlearn shame — in three stages
**Work ID:** W7211977299 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Body contradicts its own caveat on transferability** — Severity: Minor

- **Abstract says:** "Drawing on a qualitative case study of a long-standing organizational intervention supporting widowed women in India, we theorize the reduction of internalized stigma as a process of "scaffolded unlearning.""

### Study 16: Bad bosses don't just push people out — they push them against work itself
**Work ID:** W7203777671 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language for a cross-sectional survey** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this evidence points to mistreatment and abusive supervision as upstream causes, making investigation of interpersonal conduct a more useful lever than engagement messaging"
- **Abstract says:** "A preregistered survey study was conducted with a stratified sample of Swedish employees (N = 2,395). ... abusive supervision and workplace mistreatment predicted both anti-work orientation and turnover intentions."
- **Problem:** All variables are self-reported in a single cross-sectional survey, so "upstream causes" (and "can be traced to specific bad experiences" in the general-reader angle) asserts a direction the design cannot establish — contradicting the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "mistreatment and abusive supervision are associated with anti-work orientation" and note the direction of influence is untested."

**Issue B. "First" vs "among the first"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It offers the first empirical evidence that the anti-work sentiment circulating in online communities has identifiable organizational antecedents"
- **Abstract says:** "This study is among the first to empirically examine the organizational antecedents of anti-work orientation"
- **Problem:** Priority claim is stronger than the authors' own hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Among the first empirical examinations of ...""

### Study 19: Why white male managers resist diversity programs differently in Johannesburg and Chicago
**Work ID:** W7197013324 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cities not in the study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Why white male managers resist diversity programs differently in Johannesburg and Chicago"
- **Abstract says:** "a comparative case study of White men in South Africa and the United States"
- **Problem:** No cities are named in the abstract; the headline implies the research was sited in Johannesburg and Chicago specifically.
- **Suggested fix:** "Refer to South Africa and the United States in the headline."

**Issue B. Influence overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a group whose cooperation largely determines whether diversity programs get implemented"
- **Abstract says:** "a group whose sensemaking is consequential for the implementation of diversity management"
- **Problem:** "Largely determines" is a stronger causal claim about implementation than the abstract's "consequential."
- **Suggested fix:** ""a group whose interpretations matter for how diversity management gets implemented.""

**Issue C. Tensions reframed as resistance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the same resistance can spring from entirely different roots depending on a country's history"
- **Abstract says:** "Analysis of 32 middle managers shows how context-specific tensions are patterned differently across the two settings."
- **Problem:** The paper describes context-specific tensions surfaced through sensemaking analysis, not measured resistance to or obstruction of programs.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "tension" or "discomfort" rather than "resistance," matching the authors' language."
