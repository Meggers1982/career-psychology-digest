### 1. Narcissistic bosses hear the good ideas and silence the bad news

**Journal:** *Journal of Managerial Psychology* | **Published:** 2026-09-10
**Work ID:** W7212150272 | **DOI:** 10.1108/jmp-09-2025-0889

**The study:** Researchers surveyed 408 employees and 68 supervisors at Chinese firms across three time points, measuring how employees perceived their leader's narcissism and how much they spoke up at work. They separated two kinds of speaking up: promotive voice (suggestions for improvement) and prohibitive voice (flagging problems, risks and wrongdoing). Narcissistic leadership was positively associated with promotive voice but negatively associated with prohibitive voice, and both links ran through a "chaxu" team climate — one in which the leader openly divides the team into favored insiders and outsiders. Leaders who reported better work-family balance were less likely to foster that insider-outsider climate.

**Why it matters:** The leaders most likely to be praised for "energizing" their teams may be systematically starving themselves of warnings — employees pitch flashy ideas but stop reporting risks, which is precisely the information that prevents costly failures.

**Story angles:**
- **For working professionals and managers:** If your team generates plenty of ideas but almost never raises problems, treat that asymmetry as a warning sign about your own leadership style rather than evidence of a well-run operation. Managers can audit the ratio of suggestions to risk reports in their meetings, and watch for the insider-outsider dynamics — favored confidants, differentiated access — that appear to drive the silence. The study is correlational and based in Chinese organizations, so it suggests a pattern worth probing, not a diagnostic test.
- **For general readers:** It offers a read on the everyday calculation employees make around a self-focused boss: flattery-adjacent suggestions are safe, bad news is not. The finding that leaders with better work-family balance built less favoritism into their teams also hints that an overloaded manager's home life spills into how fairly they treat people.

**Caveats:** Single country (China) and the "chaxu climate" construct is culturally specific; leadership narcissism was measured through employee perceptions rather than independently assessed; time-lagged but still correlational, so causal direction is not established; voice behavior relationships may be shaped by common source across employee-reported measures.
