### Study 1: A CEO's Politics May Shape Whether a Company Builds or Buys
**Work ID:** W7221038267 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported effect magnitude** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this suggests a sizeable chunk of the variation reflects who is in the corner office and what they personally value"
- **Abstract says:** "persistent heterogeneity in vertical integration suggests that such efficiency-based explanations are incomplete... Our findings suggest that firm boundaries reflect not only transaction conditions but also systematic differences in executive decision-making."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no effect size or share of variance explained. "A sizeable chunk of the variation" converts a directional association into a quantified claim about explanatory power.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as: "this suggests some of the variation reflects who is in the corner office — the abstract does not report how much.""

**Issue B. Causal phrasing for an associational result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "using firm fixed effects and CEO successions to isolate the effect of a change in leadership"
- **Abstract says:** "We examine whether CEO political ideology is associated with systematic variation in firm boundary decisions... Using a panel of 1,198 U.S. public firms from 2001 to 2020 and a firm fixed-effects design exploiting CEO successions, we find that firms led by more conservative CEOs exhibit higher levels of vertical integration."
- **Problem:** The abstract frames the result as an association and the design as "exploiting" successions, not as isolating a causal effect. The Caveats section does walk this back, but the body text states it more strongly than the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's own wording — "a firm fixed-effects design that exploits CEO successions" — rather than "isolate the effect.""

**Issue C. Ideology measure not described in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "CEO ideology is inferred from external indicators (typically political donations), not measured directly"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract does not state how CEO political ideology was operationalized."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. The donations-based measure is a plausible standard approach but is not confirmed by the text supplied.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "CEO ideology is inferred from archival indicators rather than measured attitudes" or confirm the measure from the full text."

### Study 2: The "Liberal CEOs Do More Good" Story Looks Wrong — It's the Moderates Who Do Least
**Work ID:** W7224801151 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Advice beyond what was tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "loyalty, duty and group welfare can motivate as effectively as equality and harm-avoidance"
- **Abstract says:** "liberal- and conservative-leaning CEOs differ in their focus on individual- versus group-centric concerns but, contrary to the existing consensus, should both endorse evenhanded outcomes and pro-social corporate practices."
- **Problem:** The study examined the association between CEO ideology and corporate pro-social practices; it did not test whether differently framed appeals persuade leaders, so a comparative claim about motivational effectiveness is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as a hypothesis: "the pattern is consistent with the idea that group-centric moral language may resonate with conservative-leaning leaders — though the study did not test persuasive framing.""

**Issue B. Study design label not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Archival study of large U.S. public companies"
- **Abstract says:** "We find evidence for corresponding U-shaped rather than linear effects of CEOs' political ideology in a sample of over 1,600 S&P 1500 CEOs."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract names the sample but not the data source or design. The inference is reasonable but should be hedged.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "a large-sample study of S&P 1500 CEOs" unless the archival design is confirmed in the full text."
