### Study 2: Bossing people around feels good and bad at the same time — for the boss
**Work ID:** W7220455658 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Authoritatively" vs. authoritarian leadership** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "On days when leaders behaved more authoritatively, they felt both more pride and more guilt."
- **Abstract says:** "authoritarian leadership can simultaneously elicit feelings of pride and guilt in leaders"
- **Problem:** "Authoritative" and "authoritarian" are distinct leadership constructs; using the former risks readers thinking the study is about confident, expert-based leadership rather than controlling, domineering behavior.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "On days when leaders behaved more authoritarianly" or "On days with more authoritarian leadership behavior"."

### Study 3: Toxic workplaces don't just make work unpleasant — they make it feel pointless
**Work ID:** W7223396210 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal framing beyond "associated with"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this large longitudinal study locates its destruction in the organizational environment — and shows the damage runs through thwarted autonomy, competence and belonging"
- **Abstract says:** "ODC was prospectively associated with higher NF, which was associated with lower meaningful work and higher MLW; both longitudinal indirect associations were significant."
- **Problem:** The abstract consistently uses "associated" language for an observational panel design; "locates its destruction" and "shows the damage runs through" assert causation and mechanism more firmly than the evidence allows, partly contradicting the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "links lower meaning to the organizational environment" and "the association appears to run through frustrated needs for autonomy, competence and belonging.""

### Study 4: In a 'masculinity contest' workplace, being good at the job won't save you — being connected might
**Work ID:** W7224327719 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome drift from job satisfaction to "survival" and "thriving"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It suggests that in hyper-competitive cultures, survival depends on social standing rather than performance"
- **Abstract says:** "the relationship between MCC and job satisfaction is moderated by gender... there were no moderation effects associated with perceived in-role competence"
- **Problem:** The only outcome in the abstract is job satisfaction. Nothing in the study measures whether people survive, advance or thrive, so "survival depends on social standing" (and the angle's "who thrives") overstates what was tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "job satisfaction in hyper-competitive cultures tracks social connectedness rather than felt competence" and change "who thrives" to "who stays satisfied"."

**Issue B. Perceived competence presented as actual job competence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Crucially, feeling competent at one's actual job tasks offered no protection at all."
- **Abstract says:** "there were no moderation effects associated with perceived in-role competence, potentially reflecting that in-role performance may not buffer employees against the potential harms of MCC environments"
- **Problem:** The measure is perceived in-role competence, and the abstract hedges the inference with "potentially"; the headline's "being good at the job" and the flat "no protection at all" read as a test of real performance.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "employees' own sense of being good at their job tasks showed no buffering effect" and keep the authors' hedge."

### Study 5: What your resting heart rate says about how well you're working
**Work ID:** W7220837475 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported temporal claim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "chronic strain shows up in the body weeks before it shows up in output"
- **Abstract says:** "When HR/HRV were interpreted as secondary outcomes, lower HR, lower LFHF, and higher HRV predicted better job performance... the same physiological indicators may show opposite relationships with performance depending on the timing and context of measurement."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports within-person associations between physiological indicators and performance ratings; it does not establish that bodily signs of strain precede performance decline by weeks.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "accumulated strain registers in resting cardiac measures that track alongside weaker performance, while a pre-task surge of arousal can go with better performance.""

### Study 7: Managers judge freelancers on output, not loyalty or knowledge sharing
**Work ID:** W7213352600 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Composition of the paired sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers collected data from 489 respondents in China — freelancers and the managers who worked with them"
- **Abstract says:** "We employed paired-match approach to collect data from 489 respondents."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify who made up the matched pairs; the freelancer–manager pairing is a reasonable inference but is not stated. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "489 respondents in China using a paired-match design" or attribute the pairing explicitly as an inference."

**Issue B. "Did not mediate at all" overstates a null result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Organizational commitment and knowledge transfer did not mediate the relationship at all."
- **Abstract says:** "knowledge transfer and integration and organizational commitment do not significantly mediate this relationship"
- **Problem:** A non-significant path is not evidence of zero effect; "at all" converts a null finding into a proven absence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Organizational commitment and knowledge transfer showed no significant mediating effect.""
