### Study 1: Autonomy, competence, belonging: not just nice to have, but preconditions for well-being at work
**Work ID:** W7222931749 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped qualifier on consistency of findings** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "All three needs emerged as necessary conditions for all four well-being outcomes: without a minimum level of each, high well-being simply did not occur."
- **Abstract says:** "Across three studies, we found consistent support for the necessary role of psychological need satisfaction in all four well-being indicators, with nuanced variations in these necessity relationships."
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly flags "nuanced variations" in the necessity relationships; the digest presents a uniform, absolute result ("simply did not occur") that reads as stronger and cleaner than what is claimed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier, e.g. "All three needs emerged as necessary conditions across the four well-being outcomes, though the authors note nuanced variations in how strong each necessity relationship was.""

**Issue B. Three studies described as three employee samples** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across three employee samples they used necessary condition analysis (NCA)"
- **Abstract says:** "Across three studies, we found consistent support for the necessary role of psychological need satisfaction"
- **Problem:** The abstract says three studies; it does not specify that each was a separate employee sample or describe the samples at all.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Across three studies" and note that the abstract gives no sample details."

**Issue C. Cross-sectional characterization not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Necessary condition analysis identifies ceilings in cross-sectional data and cannot establish causation"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes NCA and three studies but does not state the design as cross-sectional."
- **Problem:** The design label is not verifiable from the abstract alone, although the caution itself is appropriately conservative.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the abstract does not report the study designs; NCA identifies ceilings rather than causal effects.""

### Study 2: Workplace FOMO drives after-hours phone checking — and it costs you recovery
**Work ID:** W7223126999 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language for within-person diary associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Workplace FOMO drives after-hours phone checking — and it costs you recovery"
- **Abstract says:** "On days when people experienced more wFoMO, they were more likely to use ICTs, which was positively associated with perceived informational benefits and negatively with employee recovery."
- **Problem:** "Drives" and "costs you" assert causation from day-level associations in self-report diary data; the digest's own caveat acknowledges the findings are correlational, so the headline contradicts it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Workplace FOMO goes with after-hours phone checking — and poorer recovery.""

**Issue B. Perceived benefits stated as objective** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The evening scroll through work messages genuinely does keep you better informed"
- **Abstract says:** "positively associated with perceived informational benefits"
- **Problem:** The outcome was self-reported perceived informational benefit, not verified information gain; "genuinely does keep you better informed" treats a perception as an objective fact.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "People felt better informed on days they used work technology in the evening — which is exactly why it is hard to stop.""

**Issue C. Unsupported next-day timing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "leaves you less recovered the next day"
- **Abstract says:** "negatively with employee recovery"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify a next-day lag for the recovery association.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "the next day" or say "is linked to poorer recovery.""

### Study 3: A good job only nudges Korean women toward parenthood if their husband pulls his weight
**Work ID:** W7222892783 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline for correlational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A good job only nudges Korean women toward parenthood if their husband pulls his weight"
- **Abstract says:** "decent work was associated with fertility intentions through WFE only when spousal support was high"
- **Problem:** "Nudges ... toward parenthood" implies both causation and actual childbearing; the study measured associations with fertility intentions in a three-wave survey.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "A good job is linked to stronger family plans for Korean women — but only when spousal support is high.""

**Issue B. Spousal support narrowed to husband's domestic labor** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "if their husband pulls his weight"
- **Abstract says:** "spousal support for work–family reconciliation"
- **Problem:** The construct is perceived spousal support for reconciling work and family, which is broader than an observed division of household labor.
- **Suggested fix:** "Refer to "perceived spousal support for balancing work and family" at least once in the body text."

### Study 4: Lenient bosses build psychological safety — until they are too lenient
**Work ID:** W7221798128 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for time-lagged survey** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Lenient bosses build psychological safety — until they are too lenient"
- **Abstract says:** "The results show an inverted U-shaped relationship between leader leniency and follower psychological safety"
- **Problem:** "Build" asserts a causal effect; the design is a time-lagged, multi-source survey tested with path modeling, which the digest's caveat correctly calls correlational.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Lenient bosses go with higher psychological safety — until they are too lenient.""

**Issue B. Unverified mechanism presented as finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Teams appear to read unlimited forgiveness as unfairness rather than safety."
- **Abstract says:** "Drawing on fairness theory and the “too-much-of-a-good-thing” perspective, this study examines the curvilinear relationship between leader leniency and follower psychological safety"
- **Problem:** Fairness theory is the framing lens, not a measured mediator; the abstract reports psychological safety as the mechanism, not perceived unfairness.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute it to theory, e.g. "The authors draw on fairness theory to explain why unlimited forgiveness may stop feeling safe.""

### Study 5: When fathers in finance take long paternity leave, the effects ripple past their own households
**Work ID:** W7223069476 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Theorized pathways stated as demonstrated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It shows that offering paternity leave is not enough; whether it changes anything depends on whether senior leaders visibly back it and line managers make it safe to use."
- **Abstract says:** "We theorize that, under certain conditions, these changes may coalesce into relational and collective experiences aligned with social cohesion beyond the workplace. We further theorize two pathways."
- **Problem:** The abstract repeatedly uses "we theorize" and "may"; the digest converts an interpretivist theoretical account into a demonstrated dependency, which is stronger than the evidence claimed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "The authors theorize that offering paternity leave may not be enough — whether it shifts norms appears to depend on top-management engagement and manager and peer support.""

**Issue B. Macro-context of male-dominated industry omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Qualitative interview study in a single industry (finance) and country (France), interpretivist rather than causal"
- **Abstract says:** "within the macro‐context of a male‐dominated industry"
- **Problem:** The abstract treats the male-dominated setting as a defining boundary condition for the findings, not just an industry label; the digest's caveat does not mention it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the findings are framed within a male-dominated industry context and may not transfer to more gender-balanced workplaces."
