### Study 1: Why "we grew sales" beats "we cut costs" — even when the dollars are identical
**Work ID:** W7214937827 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Capital allocation overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "changes how competent its author looks and how much capital follows, independent of the actual economics."
- **Abstract says:** "companies that report higher revenues are more attractive to investors... with implications for managerial decision-making, corporate communication, and capital allocation."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports perceived investor attractiveness in experiments and notes implications for capital allocation; it does not show that actual capital flows change. "How much capital follows" reads as a measured economic outcome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "changes how competent its author looks and how attractive the company seems to prospective investors.""

### Study 2: Field experiment in Indian markets: identical stalls drew 41% more customers when a woman was selling
**Work ID:** W7203695857 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Inference about the earnings gap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "meaning the well-documented gender earnings gap among microentrepreneurs likely stems from structural constraints rather than the sales interaction itself."
- **Abstract says:** "Researchers often attribute this gap to structural constraints, but pay less attention to how social interactions shape earnings... identifying customer approach as a novel mechanism that can generate economic advantages for female microentrepreneurs."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim about the relative contribution of structural versus interactional factors to the real-world gap; the experiment held structural factors constant and so cannot apportion causes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "suggesting that the sales interaction itself is not the source of the gap, which points attention back to structural constraints the experiment deliberately held constant.""

### Study 3: Feeling grateful made people pay more for green products — and blunted the money-grab reflex after climate news
**Work ID:** W7213965712 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies real purchases** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Feeling grateful made people pay more for green products"
- **Abstract says:** "a gratitude induction, compared with neutral and positive affect control conditions, increased willingness to pay for eco-labeled products and green investment allocation"
- **Problem:** Participants reported willingness to pay and made hypothetical allocations; no money changed hands. The headline reads as observed purchasing behavior.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Feeling grateful raised what people said they'd pay for green products.""

### Study 4: When new women join the board, firms are more likely to build abroad than buy abroad
**Work ID:** W7203630460 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Observation unit specified beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers analyzed 2,009 firm-year observations from Taiwanese manufacturing companies"
- **Abstract says:** "Using 2,009 observations from Taiwanese manufacturing firms"
- **Problem:** The abstract states 2,009 observations without specifying that they are firm-year observations. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "2,009 observations from Taiwanese manufacturing firms" unless the unit is confirmed in the full text."
