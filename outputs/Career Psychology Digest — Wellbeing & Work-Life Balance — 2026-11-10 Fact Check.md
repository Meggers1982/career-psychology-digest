### Study 1: The absentee boss does as much damage as the abusive one
**Work ID:** W7224911121 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample description not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a single country (Australia) and a mixed-occupation convenience sample of 315"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a three-wave survey of 315 Australian employees."
- **Problem:** The abstract states only that the sample was 315 Australian employees; it does not describe the sample as mixed-occupation or as a convenience sample. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Trim to "a single country (Australia) and a sample of 315 employees" unless the full text confirms the recruitment method."

### Study 2: Three kinds of hybrid worker sit in the same office — and only one of them is thriving
**Work ID:** W7225247976 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Profile content not stated in abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the castaways — high technology complexity with low autonomy, efficacy and learning support — had the worst outcomes on every measure"
- **Abstract says:** "The analysis revealed three distinct profiles: digital routinarians, digital castaways and resource-rich thrivers. Significant between-profile differences emerged across all outcomes. ... digital castaways exhibited the most vulnerable psychological and professional outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract names the profiles but never describes the indicator levels that define "digital castaways." The digest supplies a specific demand–resource composition that cannot be checked, and the manager-facing angle tells readers to look for exactly that pattern. "Worst outcomes on every measure" also goes slightly beyond "most vulnerable psychological and professional outcomes."
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the profile description to the full text or soften to "the castaways had the most vulnerable outcomes," and note the indicator profile is not specified in the abstract."

**Issue B. Single-university setting** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers surveyed technical and administrative staff at a public university"
- **Abstract says:** "This study investigated the heterogeneity of hybrid work experiences in the public academic sector ... A cross-sectional design involved academic technical-administrative staff engaged in hybrid work."
- **Problem:** The abstract says "public academic sector," not a single named or single public university; the number of institutions is not stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "technical and administrative staff in the public academic sector"."

**Issue C. Self-report claim for performance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "outcomes including performance are self-reported from the same source as the predictors"
- **Abstract says:** "Distal outcomes included psychological well-being, job satisfaction and multidimensional job performance."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify whether job performance was self-rated or supervisor-rated. Plausible but unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Hedge to "outcomes appear to be self-reported; the abstract does not specify the performance rating source.""

### Study 3: The four-day week's quieter cousin: what the evidence actually says about compressed schedules
**Work ID:** W7225322848 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of subjective vs. objective evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "evidence on health, fatigue, sleep, productivity and performance is genuinely mixed — especially when objective measures replace self-reports"
- **Abstract says:** "Evidence regarding health, fatigue, sleep, productivity, and performance remains mixed, particularly depending on daily working hours and whether subjective or objective indicators are utilised."
- **Problem:** The abstract says results vary by whether subjective or objective indicators are used, but does not state that objective measures produce weaker or worse results. The digest (and the manager angle, "satisfaction surveys will likely look better than fatigue or output data") implies a direction the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "and results differ depending on daily hours and on whether subjective or objective indicators are used.""

### Study 4: Neurodiversity accommodations work better in bundles than one at a time
**Work ID:** W7104623315 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies comparative effectiveness** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Neurodiversity accommodations work better in bundles than one at a time"
- **Abstract says:** "All 128 interventions were associated with at least one outcome cluster and 73.4% with multiple clusters (mean = 2.28), indicating systematic cross-cluster alignment. Outcomes rarely appeared in isolation ... The predominantly cross-sectional and geographically concentrated evidence base limits causal inference."
- **Problem:** The review maps co-occurrence of interventions and outcome clusters; it does not test bundles against single accommodations. "Work better" asserts a comparative causal effect the design cannot establish, even though the body and Caveats are properly hedged.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Neurodiversity support clusters together — the evidence points to bundles, not one-off adjustments"."
