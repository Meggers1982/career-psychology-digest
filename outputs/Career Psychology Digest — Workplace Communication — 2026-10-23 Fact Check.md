### Study 1: The 30% rule: how much diversity a group needs before it actually changes anything
**Work ID:** W7212172629 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction added to neutral outcome list** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "less misconduct, lower financial risk, innovation, reputation and performance"
- **Abstract says:** "associated with improved interpersonal dynamics with downstream effects on employee satisfaction, the success of diversity and corporate social responsibility initiatives, misconduct, financial risk, innovation, organizational reputation, and performance"
- **Problem:** The abstract names misconduct and financial risk as outcome domains without stating the direction of effect; the digest asserts these go down.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "and downstream effects on employee satisfaction, diversity and CSR initiative success, misconduct, financial risk, innovation, reputation and performance" without specifying direction."

**Issue B. Two moderators dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "effects are stronger and the threshold lower when underrepresented members hold leadership roles and when organizational values align with theirs"
- **Abstract says:** "Critical mass effects are stronger and the threshold is lower when underrepresented members hold leadership roles, organizations operate in more equitable contexts, organizational and societal values align with those of the minority group, and organizations have stronger prior performance."
- **Problem:** Two of four named boundary conditions are omitted — notably "stronger prior performance," which is directly relevant to the digest's own reverse-causality caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that effects are also stronger in more equitable contexts and in organizations with stronger prior performance."

**Issue C. Headline implies an established rule** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The 30% rule: how much diversity a group needs before it actually changes anything"
- **Abstract says:** "Among studies meeting methodologically requirements, 83% support critical mass threshold effects, with a mean threshold of 29% (SD = 6.67%)"
- **Problem:** "Rule" and "needs before it actually changes anything" frame a mean threshold from a vote-count review of largely correlational studies as an established causal rule; the SD of 6.67% and the body's own caveat about wide variation argue against this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like "Around 30%: the representation threshold where group dynamics tend to shift.""
