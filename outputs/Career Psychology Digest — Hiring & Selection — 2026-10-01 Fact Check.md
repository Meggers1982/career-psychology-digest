### Study 1: Video job tests work as well as written ones — and are easier on candidates' brains
**Work ID:** W7214648548 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null result framed as equivalence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "open-ended written responses were as valid as multiple-choice"
- **Abstract says:** "the multiple-choice modality did not significantly outperform written constructed response modalities in terms of validity"
- **Problem:** A failure to find a significant difference is not the same as demonstrated equivalence; the digest converts absence of evidence into evidence of equal validity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "multiple-choice formats did not significantly outperform written constructed-response formats on validity"."

**Issue B. Cost comparison not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "SJTs are cheap relative to assessment centers and interviews"
- **Abstract says:** "The implications of these findings are among others discussed in the context of the cost effectiveness of SJTs vis-à-vis other selection procedures."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers generically to cost effectiveness versus other selection procedures; it does not name assessment centers or interviews or quantify the comparison. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the authors discuss SJT cost effectiveness relative to other selection procedures, without naming specific methods."

### Study 2: Workers with invisible disabilities get doubted; workers with visible ones get assumed about
**Work ID:** W7211881958 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Scope widened beyond physical disability** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Workers with invisible disabilities get doubted; workers with visible ones get assumed about"
- **Abstract says:** "we screened 1871 peer‐reviewed English‐language empirical articles and retained 291 studies focused on physical disability at work"
- **Problem:** "Invisible disabilities" is commonly read to include mental health and chronic illness; this review covers only physical disability, with visibility varying within that category. The body text clarifies, but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "physical" to the headline, e.g. "Workers with less visible physical disabilities get doubted..."."

**Issue B. Hedge removed and claim broadened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The visibility of a disability consistently shaped treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "physical disability visibility consistently appears to moderate accommodation experiences"
- **Problem:** The abstract hedges ("appears to moderate") and limits the claim to accommodation experiences; the digest states it as established and generalizes to "treatment" overall.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "visibility consistently appeared to shape accommodation experiences"."

**Issue C. Recommendation not sourced to the review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The simplest corrective the review points to is asking rather than inferring, at every career stage, not just at onboarding."
- **Abstract says:** "We call for future research that integrates interpersonal, organizational, and policy‐level dynamics..."
- **Problem:** The abstract's stated implication is a research agenda, not an "ask rather than infer" practice prescription. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Present the advice as the digest's inference rather than as something the review recommends."

### Study 4: What a bad boss does to a brand-new hire: nurses describe learning to fake it, then checking out
**Work ID:** W7203635941 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Embellished detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "instrumental endurance (staying only for the paycheck or the resume line)"
- **Abstract says:** "Repeated experiences diminish this hope, resulting in resignation, instrumental endurance and relational withdrawal."
- **Problem:** The parenthetical specifics (paycheck, resume line) are not in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the parenthetical or label it as the digest's gloss on "instrumental endurance"."

**Issue B. Causal drift in framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "early mistreatment appears to convert engaged recruits into quietly disengaged ones who are hard to win back"
- **Abstract says:** "Experiences of AS lead to self-suppressive facades aimed at maintaining the hope of future resonance... Alienation emerges as a way of inhabiting the relationship"
- **Problem:** A retrospective qualitative study cannot establish conversion rates or that disengaged newcomers are "hard to win back"; the hedge "appears" only partly covers this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as what the interviews describe (a trajectory participants recounted) rather than an outcome the study documents."
